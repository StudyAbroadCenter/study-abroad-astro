# RSJP Experience System — Operational Content Rules

For the full content specification, see `docs/handbook/05_CONTENT_SPEC.md`.

For Japanese, English, Korean, locale routing, terminology, and multilingual review rules, see `docs/handbook/22_LOCALISATION_SPEC.md`.

## Core Rule

Write for prospective international students.

Every piece of content should help a student:

- understand,
- trust,
- decide,
- act.

## Accuracy

Never invent programme facts.

Verify:

- dates,
- fees,
- eligibility,
- capacity,
- application periods,
- status,
- accommodation,
- academic requirements.

If unknown, say it is unknown or not yet confirmed.

## Clear Before Clever

Prefer simple, direct language.

Avoid:

- bureaucratic wording,
- academic jargon,
- vague promotion,
- unnecessary complexity,
- excessive adjectives.

## English

English should be natural and accessible to international university students.

Do not translate Japanese literally.

Prefer short sentences, familiar vocabulary, and clear verbs.

## Japanese

Japanese should be professional, friendly, concise, and understandable.

Avoid unnecessary administrative formality.

## Translation

Preserve facts, not sentence structure.

Different language versions may differ in wording and order, but not in programme facts.

## Important Facts

Do not bury dates, fees, deadlines, status, or eligibility inside long prose.

## CTA

Prefer descriptive actions:

- Apply Now
- View Programme
- Check Dates and Fees
- See Accommodation
- Read Application Guidelines
- Contact Us

Avoid vague labels such as `Click Here`.

## Tone

Be:

- welcoming,
- confident,
- helpful,
- human,
- trustworthy.

Do not sound:

- exaggerated,
- bureaucratic,
- childish,
- aggressively promotional.

## Avoid Generic AI Copy

Avoid default patterns such as:

- Embark on...
- Immerse yourself...
- Discover the perfect blend...
- Whether you're...
- More than just...
- It's not just X — it's Y.

Specific programme reality is stronger than generic inspiration.

## Final Test

If a sentence does not help the student understand, trust, decide, or act, consider removing it.
