# Impact & Evidence publishing policy

This document defines the review-build rules for educational-impact claims on the Ritsumeikan Short-Term Programs site.

## Principle

The site should make the accumulated work visible without turning programme promotion into unsupported impact claims.

Use this hierarchy:

1. **Observed programme fact** — dates, participant counts, learning hours, activity records, buddy participation and institutions may be published when they are confirmed in programme records.
2. **Participant-reported outcome** — survey findings must be described as reported perceptions, with programme/year and sample size where a number is displayed.
3. **Interpretation** — educational concepts such as co-learning or intercultural learning may be used when the underlying activity supports the term, but should not be presented as a measured causal outcome unless an appropriate assessment exists.
4. **Long-term impact** — do not claim changes in competence, career outcomes or social impact without evidence that directly measures them.

## Review-build metrics currently used

The homepage Impact & Evidence module currently uses only the following quantitative items from the **2025 RSJP participant survey** (16 respondents from 17 participants; 94.1% response rate):

- 15/16 reported improved Japanese conversation ability (Strongly agree + Agree).
- 15/16 reported improved understanding of Japanese grammar (Strongly agree + Agree).
- 15/16 reported enough opportunities to practise speaking Japanese (Strongly agree + Agree).
- 14/16 reported that Japanese-language classes helped their understanding of life in Japan (Strongly agree + Agree).

These are programme-specific participant-reported results. They must not be converted into all-programme averages or causal claims.

## Qualitative evidence used in copy

The current module also reflects recurring themes documented in supplied surveys:

- RSJP and RWJP participants repeatedly value conversations with Ritsumeikan buddies and ask for more opportunities to interact with them.
- Survey comments request more small-group speaking, finer level placement and better balance between academic workload and cultural activities.
- 2025 RDSP participants reported learning about AI, data science, robotics, coding with physical hardware and Japanese culture; one response described increased interest in further AI study/research.

These themes are used as evidence-aware editorial summaries, not as quantified outcomes.

## Continuous improvement wording

Until a source explicitly documents both a student request and the implemented change, use wording such as:

- `学生の声を次年度設計に返す`
- `改善意見を記録し、次の設計で検討する`
- `継続的に見直す`

Do **not** use `You told us. We changed it.` for a specific item unless both sides of that statement are documented.

## Network data

Global network counts remain governed by `docs/GLOBAL_NETWORK_DATA.md`. Institution examples and survey evidence are separate evidence layers and should not be conflated.

## Public-launch checklist

Before production launch:

- reconcile displayed figures against source-of-truth programme records;
- retain programme year and respondent count with survey percentages/counts;
- confirm public naming rights and terminology for partner/participant institutions;
- remove or revise any review copy that implies measured competence or causality without a validated instrument;
- add a human review owner and last-reviewed date to the evidence dataset.

## Tone

Student-facing copy may be playful and memorable. Evidence labels, source notes and numeric claims must remain plain, precise and auditable.
