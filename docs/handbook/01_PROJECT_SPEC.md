# RSJP Experience System — Product Specification

## Vision

Create one of the strongest student-centred short-term study programme recruitment experiences in Japan.

This is not a university information portal.

It is a recruitment product designed to help prospective international students understand programmes, trust the organiser, imagine participating, and confidently decide whether to apply.

## Mission

The user should move naturally through:

1. interest,
2. understanding,
3. trust,
4. suitability assessment,
5. confidence,
6. action.

The site must combine clarity, authenticity, useful emotion, and ease of action.

## Primary Audience

Prospective international university students, typically young adults, who may:

- be considering short-term study abroad,
- be interested in Japan,
- be learning Japanese,
- use English as a second or additional language,
- compare multiple programmes,
- browse primarily on mobile,
- have limited knowledge of Japanese universities,
- need clear information about cost, accommodation, language ability, safety, and application procedures.

## Secondary Audience

- parents,
- partner university staff,
- study-abroad advisors,
- academic staff.

These users matter, but do not override the student-first information architecture.

## Product Character

The experience should feel:

- modern,
- welcoming,
- authentic,
- premium through restraint,
- trustworthy,
- international,
- student-centred,
- easy to understand.

It should not feel like:

- government administration,
- an internal university system,
- a policy document,
- a generic study-abroad template,
- a tourism campaign.

## Trust

Trust is a product requirement.

Build it through:

- accurate information,
- transparent pricing,
- clear requirements,
- clear schedules,
- honest limitations,
- authentic photography,
- consistent terminology.

Do not exaggerate benefits or hide important conditions.

## Non-Goals

The project is not intended to:

- reproduce the corporate university website,
- become a general university portal,
- publish every internal administrative detail,
- prioritise organisational hierarchy over student needs,
- introduce enterprise architecture during MVP,
- introduce CMS or user accounts before operational need,
- add features solely because competitors have them,
- maximise visual effects.

## MVP Scope

The MVP should deliver a complete recruitment experience focused on:

- programme discovery,
- programme comparison where useful,
- programme details,
- dates,
- fees,
- eligibility,
- accommodation,
- academic and cultural experience,
- location/campus,
- FAQ,
- application guidance,
- clear CTA.

## Future Expansion

Possible future capabilities may include:

- CMS,
- multilingual content management,
- analytics improvements,
- programme comparison,
- application workflow integration,
- staff editing workflows,
- reusable programme-data management.

Future possibilities must not create unnecessary present-day complexity.

## User Questions

Important pages should help answer meaningful questions such as:

- What is this programme?
- Is it right for me?
- What will I study?
- What will I experience?
- When is it?
- How much does it cost?
- Where will I stay?
- What Japanese level do I need?
- What is included?
- What happens after I apply?
- What should I do next?

## Success Evidence

Where data is available, evaluate:

- application conversion,
- completion behaviour,
- engagement with decision-critical content,
- reduction in repetitive enquiries,
- applicant feedback,
- partner feedback,
- mobile usability,
- site performance.

Do not use page views alone as success.

## Technology Direction

Preferred current architecture:

- Astro,
- TypeScript,
- GitHub,
- Vercel.

Use the repository's existing styling system.

Technology may evolve; the product principles should not depend on one framework.

## Decision Priority

1. Accuracy and trust
2. Student experience
3. Clarity
4. Accessibility
5. Performance
6. Maintainability
7. Visual polish
8. Development convenience

## Product Decision Test

Before introducing a significant feature, ask:

1. Does it help a prospective student understand or choose?
2. Does it strengthen trust?
3. Does it make the next step clearer?
4. Does it improve real UX?
5. Is there a simpler way to achieve the same result?

If the first four answers are mostly no, reconsider the feature.
