# RSJP Experience System — FAQ Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

FAQ is a **derivative clarification layer**. It does not own programme policy or facts.

Every factual answer must defer to the authoritative domain defined in `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`.

If FAQ conflicts with another view, do not merely rewrite the FAQ. Resolve the authoritative fact first, then propagate the correction.

A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

FAQ content exists to resolve real uncertainty.

It is not filler, an SEO dumping ground, or a place to hide information that should have been clear earlier.

Students should be able to resolve recurring questions, understand real exceptions, clarify conditions, confirm practical details, know when individual contact is needed, and avoid preventable misunderstandings.

**Answer real questions with real answers.**

## 2. FAQ Is a Support Layer

FAQ supports—not replaces—programme facts, eligibility, fees, accommodation, Safety & Support, application guidance, syllabus, and pre-arrival guidance.

If a fact is essential to a decision, it belongs in primary content first. FAQ may clarify or reinforce it.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Accuracy Rules

Never create FAQ answers from assumptions.

Never invent policies, exceptions, deadlines, fees, support arrangements, accommodation conditions, visa requirements, refund rules, academic requirements, application procedures, or interaction guarantees.

If an answer is not verified, mark the uncertainty appropriately, route to the correct contact when individual review is required, or omit the question until verified.

## 4. Sources for Questions

Prefer real evidence:

- email enquiries,
- application questions,
- orientation questions,
- programme/participant feedback,
- recurring staff explanations,
- partner-university questions,
- observed applicant confusion,
- operational incident patterns.

Do not create a large FAQ list simply because a site is expected to have one.

## 5. Question Prioritisation

Prioritise questions that are frequent, high impact, easy to misunderstand, or decision-critical around eligibility, money, accommodation, safety, application, language, academics, and required participation.

Do not prioritise trivial questions merely because they are easy to answer.

## 6. Categories

Possible categories include:

- Programme Overview
- Eligibility
- Japanese Language
- Academics
- Application
- Fees and Payment
- Cancellation and Refunds
- Accommodation
- Student Interaction
- Safety and Support
- Travel and Arrival
- Daily Life
- Documents
- Visa / Immigration
- After Acceptance

Create only categories containing useful content.

## 7. Programme-Specific vs Site-Wide FAQ

Use programme-specific FAQ for answers that genuinely vary by programme.

Use shared site-wide answers only when the factual answer is truly identical across programmes.

Do not force a shared answer onto different rules simply to reduce duplication.

## 8. Answer Structure

Prefer:

1. direct answer,
2. short explanation,
3. important condition/exception,
4. next action/link where needed.

When a question can be answered `Yes`, `No`, `Not necessarily`, or `It depends`, lead with that answer when accurate.

Do not bury the answer behind a long introduction.

## 9. Answer Length

Keep answers concise enough to scan.

If substantial explanation is needed, answer briefly and link to the authoritative detailed page.

Do not turn every FAQ item into a mini-policy manual.

## 10. Conditional Answers

State material conditions early.

For example, if accommodation is available only for a partner group, the answer must not begin with an unqualified `Yes`.

## 11. Unknown Answers

An unknown answer is better than a false answer.

Use clear wording such as `This has not yet been confirmed` or `Details will be announced after confirmation` only when that statement is itself true.

Do not use unexplained `TBD` when a sentence is clearer.

## 12. Exceptions and Edge Cases

FAQ is useful for real exceptions such as different application routes, group accommodation, passport renewal, late document issuance, or individual-arrival constraints.

Do not publish hypothetical exceptions or informal workarounds as policy.

## 13. Eligibility / Language / Academics

Eligibility questions must follow `18_BEFORE_YOU_APPLY_SPEC.md`.

Language/academic questions must follow `13_SYLLABUS_SPEC.md` and real programme requirements.

Do not invent external scale equivalencies or guarantee home-university credit.

## 14. Application / Fees / Refunds

Changing application facts should come from the Application Domain rather than manually duplicated FAQ values.

Financial and refund answers are high-risk. Use exact approved amounts, timing, fee scope, and refund basis.

Do not paraphrase refund rules loosely.

## 15. Accommodation

Accommodation FAQ must follow `15_ACCOMMODATION_SPEC.md` and the current offering.

Possible questions include room sharing, roommate selection, Wi-Fi, kitchen/laundry/meals, campus access, guarantee status, self-arrangement, and extensions.

Do not generalise a previous year's allocation.

## 16. Student Interaction

Questions about buddies, matching, frequency, clubs, language, and optional activities must follow `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`.

Do not answer `Will I make Japanese friends?` with a promise. Explain the real interaction opportunities instead.

## 17. Safety & Support

Safety FAQ must follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`.

Possible questions include illness, emergency action, insurance, after-hours support, hospital accompaniment, earthquake/typhoon response, and serious-concern reporting.

Do not invent emergency procedures or support coverage.

## 18. Visa / Legal / Regulatory Topics

Visa, immigration, legal, tax, customs, and similar regulated topics require current authoritative external sources when specific factual guidance is needed.

Keep answers narrow, identify the relevant external authority, and avoid interpreting beyond the programme's role.

Do not turn FAQ into informal legal advice.

## 19. Medical Topics

FAQ may explain programme process but must not diagnose, prescribe, or give personalised medical advice.

Use verified support routes and current authoritative guidance where needed.

## 20. Travel / Arrival

Questions about airport choice, pickup, arrival timing, early arrival, route, or departure must follow `19_BEFORE_YOU_ARRIVE_SPEC.md` and actual current arrangements.

Do not turn a practical route suggestion into an official requirement unless it is one.

## 21. Daily Life and Volatile Information

Questions such as cash/card use, connectivity, packing, or weather may depend on changing conditions.

Prefer current practical/official guidance rather than freezing unstable claims into permanent FAQ.

## 22. Student Responsibility

FAQ may clarify responsibilities around travel documents, medication, spending, independent travel, laundry, commuting, or communication.

Use neutral language. Responsibility is not the same as withdrawal of support.

## 23. Real Applicant Language

Phrase questions in the language students actually use where practical.

Prefer `Do I need to know Japanese?` over bureaucratic formulations such as `What linguistic prerequisites are required?`

## 24. Language and Translation

English should be direct, plain, natural, and understandable to international students.

Japanese should be concise, professional, and student-facing.

Language versions may differ in structure but must preserve the same factual answer.

## 25. Specific Question Titles

Use descriptive questions rather than label-only headings.

Prefer `Is accommodation included in the programme fee?` over `Accommodation`.

## 26. Duplicate / Contradictory Questions

Consolidate duplicate concerns.

If FAQ answers conflict with each other or another authoritative view, stop publication and resolve the underlying fact/source first.

FAQ is often where hidden policy inconsistency becomes visible; treat that as useful governance evidence.

## 27. FAQ as Operational Feedback

Repeated questions often mean:

1. the main page is unclear,
2. information is hard to find,
3. the rule itself is confusing.

Do not automatically add another FAQ. First ask whether the primary content or underlying process should improve.

## 28. Promote Important Answers Out of FAQ

If a question becomes frequent and decision-critical—such as fee, deadline, language requirement, or accommodation inclusion—move the information into primary content.

FAQ then reinforces it rather than hiding it.

## 29. Remove Obsolete Questions

FAQ is not an archive.

Remove/update questions when policy changes, the programme ends, the issue disappears, or the answer becomes inaccurate.

Do not retain obsolete items solely for SEO.

## 30. Previous-Year Reuse

Re-verify all answers dependent on dates, fees, accommodation, application, insurance, student interaction, contacts, transport, or policies.

Previous-year FAQ is reference material, not current authority.

## 31. Offering Scope

Where practical, FAQ answers should internally record programme/offering applicability so one programme/year's answer cannot silently appear in another.

## 32. Source of Truth and Verification Metadata

Important answers should trace to the relevant authoritative domain/source, such as programme offering data, syllabus, application rules, accommodation confirmation, Safety & Support procedure, approved decision, or current government guidance.

Do not use another FAQ answer as the authority.

Where practical track question ID, scope, answer owner, source, last verified date, review status, and language versions.

## 33. Ownership and FAQ Intake

FAQ should have a clear owner/process for reviewing new questions, verifying answers, removing obsolete items, and detecting contradictions.

New FAQ candidates may come from email, forms, staff notes, chat, orientation, and partner feedback.

Where practical preserve the original user question before rewriting; it reveals the actual concern.

## 34. Frequency Evidence

Do not label a question `frequently asked` internally merely because staff believe it is important.

Prioritisation may distinguish:

- frequent,
- high-risk,
- high-confusion,
- newly emerging.

Important high-risk questions may deserve FAQ coverage even when frequency is moderate.

## 35. Emerging / Temporary Questions

Policy changes, disruptions, system outages, or travel events may create temporary questions.

Use temporary notices/FAQ when appropriate and give them a review/expiry point. Do not let temporary content become permanent through neglect.

## 36. Search and Filtering

Add FAQ search/filter only when volume justifies it.

Search should account for real question wording and common synonyms.

Do not add an AI-answer layer that invents or overrides the verified underlying content.

## 37. Accordion Use

Accordions may improve scanning, but:

- essential decision facts must exist in primary content,
- controls must be semantic/keyboard accessible,
- expanded/collapsed state must be exposed accessibly,
- the whole site should not become hidden inside accordions.

## 38. Deep Links

Where practical, give stable links to individual answers for email support and partner/student reference.

Prefer stable IDs over translated question text when possible.

## 39. Contact Escalation

Some questions need individual review because of personal circumstances, sensitive information, accessibility, health, immigration status, or case-by-case policy.

Provide what can safely be stated generally, then explain what requires contact.

`Contact us` alone is not a useful answer when a general factual explanation can be provided first.

## 40. Decision Boundaries

FAQ should distinguish:

- general programme information,
- formal programme rule,
- individual programme decision,
- decision controlled by an external authority.

Examples: visa approval, home-university credit, and insurance-claim outcome are not decisions the programme can guarantee.

## 41. Safety-Critical and Financial Answers

Safety-critical answers should lead with what to do first, who to contact, and the important limitation.

Financial answers should state authoritative currency, fee scope, refund basis, and timing.

Avoid permanent approximate FX conversions unless a controlled current conversion mechanism exists.

## 42. Application-System Answers

If the application system changes, review related FAQ immediately.

Do not allow FAQ to point to an old form or describe obsolete system behaviour.

## 43. Tone

Clear, calm, direct, respectful, and helpful.

It is acceptable to say `No` when the rule is no.

Do not soften a restriction until it becomes ambiguous.

Do not hide an accurate but unattractive condition because it may reduce applications.

Trust is more valuable than short-term conversion.

## 44. No Marketing Evasion

If a student asks `Is the room shared?`, answer the room arrangement.

Do not replace the answer with a sales claim about making friends.

Avoid fake reassurance such as `Don't worry`, `Everything is taken care of`, `You'll be fine`, or `Japan is very safe`.

FAQ is not a testimonial format; never create fictional student voices.

## 45. Content Order

A programme FAQ may prioritise roughly:

1. Eligibility
2. Application
3. Fee / Payment
4. Accommodation
5. Academics
6. Student Interaction
7. Safety / Support
8. Travel / Arrival
9. Other practical questions

Adjust based on actual enquiry evidence.

## 46. Mobile / Desktop / Accessibility

At 390px questions should be easy to scan with comfortable tap targets and simple navigation. At 320px remain fully usable.

Desktop may use category navigation or search when scale warrants it; do not build a dense knowledge-base interface for a small set.

Accordion controls must use semantic interactive elements, visible focus, keyboard operation, correct state, and screen-reader-friendly labels.

## 47. Performance

FAQ should remain lightweight. Do not use a heavy client library where native/simple interaction is sufficient.

## 48. SEO

FAQ may support search when it genuinely answers user questions.

Do not keyword-stuff, fabricate questions for SEO, duplicate the same answer across many pages, or create hundreds of thin FAQ pages.

Use structured data only when current search-engine guidance supports it and the visible content exactly matches.

## 49. Analytics / Feedback

If approved, useful signals may include most-opened questions, unanswered searches, repeat contact after viewing, and high-exit items.

High opens may indicate poor primary content rather than a successful FAQ.

A `Was this helpful?` prompt is useful only when feedback will actually be reviewed.

## 50. Improvement Loop

1. Capture the real question.
2. Identify the authoritative source.
3. Decide whether primary content should change.
4. Draft the answer.
5. Verify it.
6. Publish.
7. Observe recurring confusion.
8. Revise or remove.

FAQ should evolve from evidence.

## 51. Contradiction Audit

Before major programme launch, audit FAQ against Programme Page, Syllabus, Accommodation, Safety & Support, Application, Pre-Arrival, Comparison, and current structured data.

Any contradiction blocks publication until resolved upstream.

## 52. Audience Layers

Some content may target prospective students, accepted students, partner-university staff, parents/families, or accompanying staff.

Do not overload one answer with every audience's operational detail.

Keep the primary public experience student-first. Accepted-student or partner-only details may need a separate layer/access route.

## 53. Public vs Restricted Information

Do not publish private contact trees, personal student data, confidential procedures, or access information merely because it helps internal operations.

## 54. Publication Lifecycle

Use:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible blocking review flags:

- `Cross-Programme Consistency Check Required`
- `External Source Recheck Required`
- `Translation Review Required`

Useful questions do not become publishable until the answer is verified.

## 55. Change Control

Material FAQ changes include eligibility, dates, fees, refunds, accommodation, application, Safety & Support, insurance, visa/entry information, and academic requirements.

A material change should trigger review of the underlying authoritative content, not only the FAQ wording.

## 56. Publication Readiness

FAQ is ready when questions are useful, answers verified, source/ownership known where practical, high-risk answers reviewed appropriately, language versions equivalent, obsolete items removed, and no answer conflicts with primary content.

## 57. Quality Review Checklist

### Truth
- every factual answer traces to a verified source/domain,
- assumptions and old-year answers are not presented as current,
- conditions are explicit.

### Relevance
- each question reflects a real/high-value concern,
- essential facts are promoted into main content,
- duplicates are consolidated.

### Clarity
- answer comes first,
- conditions/exceptions are visible,
- language is understandable to international students.

### Consistency / Risk
- FAQ matches current programme/application/academic/accommodation/safety data,
- visa/legal/medical/financial answers respect authoritative boundaries,
- multilingual facts match.

### UX / Governance
- mobile/accordion accessibility works,
- ownership and verification state are known,
- obsolete items are removed,
- contradiction audit is complete.

## 58. Definition of Done

FAQ is complete when questions come from real user need or genuine decision risk, answers are verified, essential information remains in primary content, conditions are explicit, high-risk topics use authoritative sources, obsolete/duplicate items are removed, programme differences are preserved, accessibility/mobile quality are strong, and contradiction checks are complete.

## 59. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> That was the question I actually had.  
> I found the answer quickly.  
> The answer was clear.  
> I understand the condition or exception.  
> I know what to do next.  
> I trust it because it matches the rest of the site.

The best FAQ is not the one with the most questions.

**It is the one that removes the most real uncertainty with the fewest unverified words.**
