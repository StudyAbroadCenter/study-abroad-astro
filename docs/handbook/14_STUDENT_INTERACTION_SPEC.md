# RSJP Experience System — Student Interaction Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs the **Student Interaction Domain**.

Current offering facts must come from the approved interaction plan or the responsible current owner/source. It does not own programme dates/fees, accommodation, formal safety responsibility, or application rules.

Follow `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

Student interaction can be one of the strongest parts of a short-term programme and one of the easiest areas to overstate.

The system should help students understand:

- what kinds of interaction exist,
- how structured or informal they are,
- what is guaranteed and what is not,
- what students are expected to contribute,
- how interaction supports learning and belonging,
- what limitations apply.

**Show the opportunity. Do not promise the relationship.**

## 2. Why This Area Requires Special Care

Interaction can create friendship, belonging, cultural exchange, language practice, confidence, and insight into student life—but these outcomes depend on individual people and circumstances.

The site may describe a real opportunity confidently. It must not guarantee personal or emotional outcomes.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Accuracy Rules

Never claim or imply, unless formally guaranteed:

- every participant receives a personal buddy,
- matching is one-to-one,
- friendships are guaranteed,
- interaction happens frequently or daily,
- local students accompany participants outside official activities,
- club participation is guaranteed,
- local students provide private/emergency support,
- all local students speak English,
- all participants have the same social experience.

Never invent matching ratios, frequency, participation numbers, schedules, testimonials, or social outcomes.

## 4. Terminology

Use the term that matches the actual model.

### Buddy Programme
A formal matching arrangement with defined organisation and responsibilities. Use the term only when matching genuinely occurs.

### Peer Exchange
Structured interaction without necessarily assigning individual partners.

### Joint / Collaborative Learning
Academic activity where visiting and Ritsumeikan students learn together through classes, projects, workshops, seminars, discussion, or presentations.

### Student-Led Activity
An activity organised or supported by student groups. Do not imply institutional guarantees beyond the real arrangement.

### Student Club Interaction
Interaction with a club/circle. A visit or exchange is not club membership.

### Social Interaction Opportunity
An opportunity to meet socially without formal matching.

### Student Support
Peer or operational support by students. Do not confuse this with staff, emergency, medical, counselling, immigration, or academic-advising responsibility.

## 5. Recommended Public Umbrella Term

When several models exist, prefer **Student Interaction** or an accurate student-facing heading such as `Meet Ritsumeikan Students`, `Learn Together`, or `Connect on Campus`.

Do not use `Buddy` as the universal label unless every relevant programme genuinely uses the same formal model.

## 6. Potential Objectives

Where supported by the actual programme, interaction may help students practise communication, understand student life, exchange perspectives, learn collaboratively, build confidence, or ask practical questions.

Describe these as opportunities/possible benefits, not guaranteed outcomes.

## 7. Academic vs Social Interaction

Clearly distinguish:

- academically required,
- programme-organised,
- optional,
- social,
- informal.

Academic interaction may involve outcomes, tasks, attendance, or instructor oversight. Social interaction may be voluntary or unstructured. Do not blur the categories to make the programme sound more intensive.

## 8. Formal Buddy Information

If a genuine buddy programme exists, explain where relevant:

- who buddies are,
- how matching works,
- individual vs group matching,
- whether matching is guaranteed,
- expected role/frequency,
- structured activities,
- communication outside programme hours,
- what buddies do not provide,
- what happens if a match does not work well.

Do not promise what operational staff cannot reliably deliver.

## 9. Matching

Only describe real criteria such as language interest, academic interest, hobbies, or availability.

Do not promise perfect matching. If the programme aims to match using available information, say so only when operationally true.

## 10. Matching Ratios

Do not assume 1:1. The model may be 1:1, 1:2, 2:2, group, or flexible. If it varies, say so.

## 11. Availability

Local-student participation can be affected by semester dates, exams, holidays, work, internships, and personal schedules. Where this materially affects the experience, explain it honestly.

Do not imply identical year-round availability.

## 12. Local Student Recruitment and Orientation

Structured programmes should internally define recruitment, eligibility, expectations, schedule, training/orientation, responsible staff, communication, privacy, cultural sensitivity, safeguarding, boundaries, and escalation.

Not every operational detail belongs on the public site, but public claims must reflect the real model.

## 13. Visiting Student Orientation

Visiting students should understand what interaction exists, what is required/optional, respectful communication, role boundaries, what to do if uncomfortable, and where official staff support begins.

## 14. Role Boundaries

Local students are not automatically:

- tour guides,
- translators,
- emergency contacts,
- counsellors,
- academic advisors,
- immigration advisors,
- medical support providers,
- 24-hour companions.

Do not shift institutional responsibility onto students.

## 15. Emergency Responsibility

Buddy/peer systems must not make students responsible for formal emergencies unless a specific limited role is formally assigned.

Official emergency and safeguarding responsibility belongs to the designated programme/staff system. Follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`.

## 16. Communication Channels

Use approved communication methods where such guidance exists. Do not assume all students use LINE, WhatsApp, Instagram, email, or another platform.

## 17. Privacy and Consent

Do not expose personal phone numbers, social handles, private email addresses, or unnecessary matching information.

Photos, names, quotes, and stories require appropriate permission. Participation in an activity is not automatic marketing consent.

## 18. Safeguarding and Respect

Where formal interaction is organised, address foreseeable boundaries appropriately, including harassment, discrimination, privacy, social pressure, off-programme meetings, personal contact sharing, alcohol, and nightlife.

Do not invent rules beyond approved policy, but do not ignore known boundary risks.

## 19. Inclusivity

Do not assume gender, nationality, sexuality, religion, family background, language confidence, extroversion, or social preferences.

Do not require students to perform one stereotype of `international friendship`.

## 20. Language Reality

Do not imply all interaction occurs in fluent English or Japanese unless true.

Interaction may be Japanese, English, mixed-language, simple language, or include appropriate translation tools. Imperfect communication can be part of authentic exchange.

## 21. Language Exchange

Use `language exchange` only when it is a structured element. Explain purpose, language balance, format, frequency, and required/optional status where relevant.

## 22. Social Outcomes

Use language such as `may`, `can`, `offers an opportunity to`, or `gives students a chance to`.

Avoid `you will make lifelong friends` or equivalent promises.

## 23. Student Stories

Use genuine approved stories and programme context only. Never generate fictional interaction stories or rewrite quotes into generic marketing copy.

## 24. Photography and Captions

Prefer authentic conversation, collaborative activity, joint learning, campus participation, and natural groups.

Avoid staged friendship imagery that implies a stronger model than exists.

Captions must accurately describe the activity. Do not label every student photo `Buddy Activity`.

## 25. Programme Page Integration

Summarise:

- interaction type,
- structured/informal nature,
- required/optional status,
- realistic expectations.

Create a separate shared page only when enough verified content justifies it.

## 26. Programme-Specific Variation

Programmes may legitimately have formal buddy matching, several peer sessions, one joint class, student-led activities, or no structured interaction.

Do not make weaker models appear equivalent to stronger ones.

## 27. Interaction Intensity and Frequency

Use actual structure: `one scheduled exchange`, `weekly joint class`, `several activities`, etc.

Avoid arbitrary scores such as `Student Interaction 5/5` or unsupported phrases such as `frequent interaction`.

## 28. Optional Events

Label optional events clearly. If availability depends on local-student interest, say so where material.

Do not imply every participant experiences optional activity.

## 29. Attendance, Cost, and Transport

If an interaction is academically required, reflect attendance in the academic/programme rules.

If it costs money or occurs off campus, explain participant responsibility where relevant. Do not assume social activity is free or transport is provided.

## 30. Alcohol and Nightlife

Do not use alcohol/nightlife as the primary representation of interaction. Independent social activity is not an official programme activity unless it genuinely is organised as such.

## 31. Staff Presence

Distinguish staff-led, faculty-led, student-led, and independently arranged interaction. Do not imply supervision where none exists or unsupervised freedom where supervision is required.

## 32. Joint Learning

Explain the real academic purpose, for example comparative discussion, collaborative problem solving, intercultural communication, presentations, or peer feedback, only where genuinely supported.

## 33. Reciprocity

Ritsumeikan students are not service providers or props.

Describe exchange as reciprocal where true: both groups contribute, share perspectives, and participate.

## 34. Student Agency

Meaningful interaction may require active participation. Explain realistic expectations such as joining conversations or attending activities without blaming students for social outcomes.

## 35. Cultural Sensitivity and Representation

Do not treat a small group of local students as representatives of all Japanese people.

Avoid claims such as `learn what Japanese people really think`.

One testimonial does not represent every participant.

## 36. Accessibility and Social Preference

Consider mobility, sensory environment, communication needs, physical activity, and venue access where relevant.

Do not stigmatise students who prefer less social intensity. Optional means optional; academically required interaction must explain why.

## 37. Conflict / Mismatch / No-Show

Formal matching should have an internal escalation route for poor fit, crossed boundaries, non-response, or withdrawal.

Do not guarantee replacement unless the programme truly guarantees one. Public promises must reflect operational fallback reality.

## 38. Capacity and Calendar Constraints

Do not advertise an interaction promise beyond the available local-student capacity.

Account for academic calendars before promising high-intensity exchange.

Do not design the public promise first and solve participant availability later.

## 39. Operational Ownership

Every structured interaction activity should have a real responsible owner/process. If ownership is unknown, resolve it before making strong public promises.

## 40. Verification Metadata and Data Model

Where practical track:

- interaction type,
- programme/offering,
- required/optional,
- guaranteed/not guaranteed,
- frequency,
- matching model,
- capacity dependency,
- language context,
- owner,
- source/approval,
- last verified date,
- current status,
- images/stories.

This is conceptual. Do not create unused fields or invent metadata.

## 41. Shared Facts and Previous-Year Reuse

Do not manually duplicate interaction facts inconsistently across programme pages, FAQ, syllabus, or student-experience content.

Last year's successful activity is not this year's commitment. Re-verify local availability, matching model, frequency, group, schedule, capacity, terminology, and guarantees.

## 42. Draft vs Confirmed / Change

An interaction may be proposed, under coordination, confirmed, completed, or cancelled. Public wording must match the real state.

If an advertised interaction is materially weakened or changed, communicate honestly where necessary.

## 43. Programme Comparison

Compare factual structures such as formal buddy matching, joint learning, peer exchange, and student-led activities.

Do not reduce complex interaction to a quality score.

## 44. FAQ Topics

Useful questions may include:

- Will I have a buddy?
- Is matching guaranteed?
- How often will I meet Ritsumeikan students?
- Are activities required?
- What language will we use?
- Can I contact a buddy before arrival?
- What happens if a buddy does not reply?
- Can I join clubs?

Answer only with verified programme-specific information.

## 45. Public Content Structure

A student-facing section/page may include:

1. Introduction
2. Interaction model
3. How it works
4. What is guaranteed
5. What is not guaranteed
6. What students can expect
7. What students contribute
8. Authentic examples/photos
9. FAQ
10. Programme-specific notes

Use only the sections needed.

## 46. UI and CTA Guidance

Prefer large real photography, editorial storytelling, factual callouts, and simple steps/timelines where useful.

Avoid gamified friendship graphics, hearts everywhere, fake chat screenshots, profile-card grids implying guaranteed matches, and stock multicultural friendship imagery.

Possible CTAs include `See How Student Interaction Works`, `Meet Ritsumeikan Students`, `View Programme Details`, and `Read the FAQ`.

Avoid promises such as `Find Your Japanese Best Friend`.

## 47. Tone

Warm, human, realistic, inviting, and respectful.

Avoid sentimental guarantees, forced enthusiasm, stereotypes, dating-app language, and social pressure.

## 48. Success Measures

If evaluation exists, useful evidence may include participation, feedback, operational completion, recurring concerns, and matching success where relevant.

Do not invent satisfaction metrics.

## 49. Publication Lifecycle

Use the canonical lifecycle:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible review flags:

- `Operational Review Required`
- `Cross-Programme Consistency Check Required`

## 50. Quality Review Checklist

Verify:

### Truth
- interaction model is correctly named,
- `Buddy` is used only for a real formal model,
- guarantees, frequency, capacity, and optional status are accurate,
- no friendship/social outcome is guaranteed.

### Student Understanding
- students can tell what interaction involves, what is required, what local students do, what they do not do, and what depends on individual participation.

### Operations
- a responsible owner exists,
- the advertised structure can actually be delivered,
- local availability is confirmed where needed,
- escalation exists for formal matching.

### Safety / Boundaries
- staff responsibility is distinct from student roles,
- privacy/consent are protected,
- emergency responsibility is not shifted to peers.

### Language / Culture
- communication reality is honest,
- local students are treated as individuals, not cultural representatives.

### UX / Governance
- photos and UI reflect the real model,
- important facts are visible on mobile,
- previous-year information is re-verified,
- current status is known.

## 51. Definition of Done

Student Interaction content is complete when the model is accurately named, guarantees/limitations are clear, expectations are realistic, responsibilities are distinct, privacy/consent are respected, imagery/testimony is authentic, optional/required status is clear, previous-year arrangements are re-verified, and no social outcome is manufactured.

## 52. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> I understand how I may meet and interact with Ritsumeikan students.  
> I know what the programme organises and what depends on individual participation.  
> I know what is guaranteed and what is not.  
> I understand that meaningful interaction is an opportunity, not a manufactured promise.  
> I feel excited because the opportunity is real.

The strongest message is not `We will give you Japanese friends.`

It is: **We create real opportunities for students to meet, learn, and participate together.**
