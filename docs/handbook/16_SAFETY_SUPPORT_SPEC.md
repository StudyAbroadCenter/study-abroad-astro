# RSJP Experience System — Safety & Support Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs the **Safety & Support Domain**.

Programme-controlled safety facts must come from current approved operational procedures. Externally controlled facts must use current authoritative external sources.

Accommodation logistics belong to `15_ACCOMMODATION_SPEC.md`; student social roles belong to `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`; accepted-student arrival operations belong to `19_BEFORE_YOU_ARRIVE_SPEC.md`.

Apply `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

Safety and support information must help students understand:

- what support systems actually exist,
- who is responsible for what,
- what to do in different situations,
- where programme responsibility begins and ends,
- what students manage independently,
- what emergency routes exist,
- what is not guaranteed.

The goal is not to imply that nothing can go wrong. It is to make the support structure understandable before something goes wrong.

**Reassurance from preparedness. Trust from defined responsibility.**

## 2. Communication Philosophy

Safety content should be calm, practical, specific, honest, proportionate, and actionable.

Avoid fear-based language, vague reassurance, absolute guarantees, excessive legalism, and implied 24-hour service that does not exist.

Good safety communication does not say `Nothing will happen.` It says `If something happens, here is what to do and who is responsible.`

## 3. Non-Negotiable Accuracy Rules

Never claim or imply, unless formally true:

- the programme or Japan is completely safe/crime-free,
- staff are available 24/7,
- staff accompany every student to hospital,
- accommodation staff provide programme emergency support,
- buddies are emergency contacts,
- insurance covers every situation,
- all hospitals provide English service,
- students will always receive immediate assistance,
- programme staff can make medical decisions,
- police, ambulance, hospital, insurance, or treatment outcomes are guaranteed.

Never invent emergency numbers, duty schedules, insurance coverage, medical procedures, evacuation arrangements, referral hospitals, response times, counselling services, or emergency transport.

## 4. Public Information vs Internal Protocol

### Public Safety Information
Students need clear information about support routes, first actions, insurance requirements, programme support, and their own responsibilities.

### Internal Operational Protocol
Staff may need escalation chains, call trees, incident logs, management notification, reporting thresholds, vendor coordination, and internal approvals.

Do not expose unnecessary internal detail publicly. Do not hide student-critical actions inside internal manuals.

## 5. Responsibility Model

Possible responsible parties include programme staff, university staff, accommodation staff, insurer, healthcare provider, emergency services, student, and partner/accompanying staff.

Responsibility must be explicit. Physical proximity does not create responsibility.

## 6. Official Support Route

Where operationally required, define official routes for:

- routine support,
- urgent support,
- public emergency services.

Prefer approved role-based channels over personal staff numbers where practical. Do not publish personal contact details without approval.

## 7. Emergency / Urgent / Routine

Where useful distinguish:

- **Emergency:** immediate threat to life, serious injury, fire, major disaster, or similarly critical situation.
- **Urgent:** serious issue needing prompt support but not an immediate life-threatening emergency.
- **Routine:** matters that can wait for normal support hours.

Use these categories only if students are given a clear action for each.

## 8. Public Emergency Services

Emergency-service information must be current and location-appropriate.

Do not rely on memory. Do not mix public emergency numbers with programme contacts. In a life-threatening situation, public emergency services may be the first appropriate route.

## 9. Support Hours and After-Hours Support

State real support hours.

Do not use `24/7 support` unless a sustainable assigned service exists, students know how to reach it, and response expectations are defined.

If after-hours support exists, explain what it is for and what it does not cover. If it does not exist, do not imply immediate response outside working hours.

## 10. Student Independence

Students may be expected to manage routine illness, pharmacy visits, minor transport disruption, ordinary accommodation issues, personal belongings, daily medication, communication, and following official instructions.

Independence is not the absence of support. The programme is not automatically a concierge service.

## 11. Medical Emergencies

In a serious or life-threatening medical event, students should use the appropriate official emergency route.

Programme staff may support communication, coordination, or follow-up only as operationally defined. Staff do not replace doctors, paramedics, hospitals, or insurers.

## 12. Non-Emergency Medical Care

Where useful explain the actual process for fever, minor injury, non-emergency illness, prescription needs, or routine consultation.

Only list clinics/hospitals/services that are verified and current enough to be useful. Do not promise English-language medical support unless confirmed.

## 13. Hospital Accompaniment

Do not promise staff accompaniment unless it is a formal commitment.

If accompaniment is conditional, describe the condition accurately. Do not shift the responsibility to buddies or other students.

## 14. Ambulance

Do not imply staff permission is needed to call an ambulance in a genuine emergency.

Public guidance should prioritise appropriate emergency action. Staff response procedures belong in internal guidance.

## 15. Insurance

Clarify verified information such as:

- whether insurance is mandatory,
- who arranges it,
- whether it is included,
- coverage period,
- major limitations where appropriate,
- claim responsibility,
- whether payment may be required before reimbursement.

Do not simplify complex policy coverage inaccurately. Link to current authoritative policy/insurer information when appropriate.

## 16. Insurance Limits and Claims

Never state that insurance covers all medical care, pre-existing conditions, activities, belongings, or cancellations unless the policy explicitly does.

Students should understand their role in contacting the insurer, retaining receipts, obtaining documents, and filing claims where that is the real process.

Do not promise programme staff will complete claims for students unless formally true.

## 17. Medical Payment

Where relevant explain that payment at the point of care may be required depending on circumstances.

Do not promise direct billing or invent expected costs.

## 18. Medication

Do not give individual medical advice or legal import advice from memory.

Do not assume a medication may be brought into Japan, a foreign prescription can be refilled, or an equivalent product is available.

Where needed use current authoritative Japanese government/customs/health guidance and encourage appropriate advance planning.

## 19. Pre-Existing Conditions / Individual Health Needs

Do not promise that every medical need can be supported.

Where participation, accommodation, or emergency planning may be affected, provide a confidential route for advance consultation. Do not request unnecessary medical detail publicly.

## 20. Mental Wellbeing

Describe counselling, English-language support, crisis support, or other services only when they are current and available to programme participants.

Do not improvise crisis procedures or represent programme staff as clinical providers.

## 21. Accessibility Support

Provide a real route for students to discuss participation needs where appropriate.

Do not promise all facilities, activities, or modifications are available. Use case-by-case assessment where that is the real process.

## 22. Allergies and Dietary Risk

Do not guarantee allergen-free environments unless verified.

Distinguish provided meals, optional meals, independent dining, and shared kitchens. Serious needs should have an appropriate confidential advance-discussion route.

## 23. Infectious Illness

Use current approved procedures only.

Do not automatically reuse pandemic-era masking, isolation, testing, or exclusion rules without a current basis.

## 24. Natural Disasters and Severe Weather

Japan may experience earthquakes, typhoons, heavy rain, heat, and other hazards.

Focus on preparedness, official information, programme communication, and appropriate action. Avoid dramatic or fear-based language.

Do not invent evacuation procedures or imply hazards are predictable.

## 25. Earthquakes

Use approved official safety principles. Venue/accommodation/campus procedures may differ.

Students should follow responsible venue staff and official authorities. Do not invent meeting points or evacuation routes.

## 26. Typhoons / Severe Weather / Heat

Programme activity may change because of weather, closures, transport disruption, or heat risk.

Use official information and defined communication channels. Do not promise normal delivery when conditions make it unsafe.

For heat-related guidance, use practical approved public-health advice rather than individual treatment advice.

## 27. Transport Disruption

Where rules exist, explain how disruption affects classes, meeting points, field activities, or accommodation return.

Do not guarantee all transport-related lateness will be excused unless that is actual policy.

## 28. Lost Property / Passport / Phone / Wallet

Explain real routes where useful, such as venue, station, police, accommodation, programme support, consular guidance, bank/card provider, or device account security.

Do not promise recovery. Lost-passport procedures must use current official consular information. Staff cannot secure private financial accounts on a student's behalf.

## 29. Crime and Police

Do not market Japan as crime-free.

If theft, assault, harassment, or another crime occurs, direct students to appropriate official and programme support routes.

Do not promise police outcomes or discourage contacting police when appropriate.

## 30. Harassment / Serious Concerns

Students should have a real reporting route for harassment, discrimination, stalking, threats, inappropriate behaviour, sexual harassment/violence, and other serious concerns where applicable.

Use respectful, non-blaming language. Do not force students to resolve serious matters informally with peers first. Protect privacy and disclose information only as operationally necessary.

## 31. Safeguarding and Minors

Where minors or other safeguarding considerations apply, responsibilities must be separately and formally defined.

Do not assume ordinary adult procedures or advertise supervision that is not actually provided.

## 32. Student Interaction Boundaries

Buddies, peers, clubs, and student organisations are not substitutes for programme staff, emergency services, medical professionals, counsellors, or security.

Remain consistent with `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`.

## 33. Accommodation Incidents and Fire

Distinguish property issues, programme issues, and emergencies.

Examples may include lost key, water leak, noise, illness, fire alarm, and security concern.

Students should know which channel to use. Follow actual property fire/evacuation procedures; do not invent meeting points.

## 34. Programme and Optional Activities

Risk differs across classroom activity, walking, field visits, cultural workshops, excursions, and transport.

Material-risk activities require operational planning before promotion.

Independent free-time activity may fall outside the same staff oversight or insurance arrangements. Do not imply programme responsibility for all personal travel/leisure choices.

## 35. Free Time, Alcohol, and Prohibited Substances

Programme support does not imply continuous supervision unless explicitly defined.

Alcohol/drug-related wording must reflect actual programme rules and current applicable law. Do not promote alcohol as a programme benefit or provide casual legal claims from memory.

## 36. Personal Safety Guidance

Practical guidance may include awareness, valuables, official transport, and communication expectations where relevant.

Avoid paternalistic or alarmist language.

Do not state a curfew unless one exists.

## 37. Emergency Communication

Define real channels for urgent information: email, messaging platform, phone, emergency app, accommodation announcement, etc.

Do not list unmonitored channels.

Internal planning may include backup routes, but the public instruction should remain simple enough to act on.

## 38. Contact Information and Privacy

Handle student contact details and personal emergency-contact information according to approved privacy practices.

Do not expose participant lists or contact information unnecessarily. Family contact should follow an operational/policy basis.

## 39. Partner / Accompanying Staff

Where partner-university staff accompany a group, define responsibilities before programme delivery.

Do not assume accompanying staff automatically accept all emergency, welfare, or personal-support responsibilities.

## 40. Incident Reporting and Privacy

Internal protocols should define what is recorded, by whom, when escalation is required, and who receives the report.

Do not skip documentation merely because an issue appears resolved. Safety response does not eliminate privacy obligations.

## 41. Media / External Communication

Serious incidents may require authorised institutional communication. Do not promise a particular public response unless procedures define it.

## 42. Support Limits

Programme staff may not be able to provide medical treatment, legal advice, guaranteed interpretation, reimbursement, lost-property recovery, private transport, accompaniment to every routine appointment, or resolution of independent travel problems.

Clear limits strengthen trust.

## 43. Escalation / Missing or Uncontactable Student

Internal escalation may be triggered by life-threatening events, hospitalisation, police involvement, missing participant, safeguarding concern, major disruption, accommodation incident, or disaster.

If an uncontactable-student protocol exists, define reasonable checks and responsibilities internally.

Do not imply continuous location monitoring unless it truly exists and is lawful.

## 44. Location Tracking and Data Minimisation

Do not require continuous location tracking without explicit programme basis, consent, privacy review, and lawful use.

Collect only safety-related data with a legitimate operational purpose.

## 45. Safety and Accessibility

Emergency planning should consider mobility, communication, sensory, medication, and other relevant needs.

Do not assume one emergency procedure works identically for every participant.

## 46. Pre-Arrival / Orientation Safety Content

Before arrival students may need verified information on insurance, medication, emergency contacts, disaster preparedness, health considerations, and communication channels.

Orientation should prioritise actions: how to seek help, what counts as emergency, how communication works, what support exists, and what responsibility remains with the student.

## 47. Emergency Quick Reference / Offline Access

A concise emergency reference may include public emergency route, programme urgent contact, insurer contact, and accommodation contact where relevant.

Only create it after contacts are final and verified.

Where practical provide critical information in a form students can retain offline; do not assume Wi-Fi, mobile data, or battery will always be available.

## 48. Safety Content Layers

Use a layered model:

1. **Recruitment Page** — high-level systems and boundaries.
2. **Safety & Support Page** — practical overview.
3. **Pre-Arrival Guide** — detailed procedures and preparation.
4. **Emergency Quick Reference** — concise actionable information.
5. **Internal Staff Protocol** — detailed operational response.

Do not place all layers on one public page.

## 49. Programme Page Integration

Programme pages may summarise insurance requirement, support route, emergency principle, and key student responsibility.

Do not turn programme pages into safety manuals.

## 50. Public Page Structure

A dedicated Safety & Support page may include:

1. How Support Works
2. Emergency vs Routine Support
3. Medical Care
4. Insurance
5. Mental Wellbeing
6. Natural Disasters
7. Accommodation Safety
8. Harassment / Serious Concerns
9. Student Responsibilities
10. Important Contacts
11. FAQ

Publish only verified/relevant sections.

## 51. Tone / Visual Design / Warning Hierarchy

Sound calm, competent, practical, respectful, and transparent.

Avoid `Don't worry`, `Japan is extremely safe`, `We will take care of everything`, or `You are never alone` unless specifically true and appropriate.

Use clear hierarchy, simple icons only where useful, calm visual treatment, and strong action/contact blocks. Avoid hazard-striping aesthetics and dramatic emergency imagery.

Use strong warning styling only for truly urgent information. A possible hierarchy is `Information`, `Important`, `Urgent / Emergency`.

## 52. Maps and Multilingual Safety Content

Use maps only when current and practically useful.

Safety-critical meaning must remain equivalent across languages. Do not simplify away responsibility, urgency, limitations, insurance conditions, or actions.

Prioritise actionable clarity over literal translation.

## 53. Readability and Accessibility

Use short sentences, direct verbs, clear labels, and numbered steps when sequence matters.

Critical instructions must not rely on colour, icons, images, or animation alone and must work with assistive technologies.

## 54. Source of Truth and Verification

Safety-critical facts should come from approved university/programme procedures, insurer documents, accommodation provider information, government/local authority sources, and emergency-service guidance as applicable.

Do not treat informal staff memory as sufficient authority for critical public information.

Where practical track owner, source, last verified date, offering, support hours, contact validity, and policy version.

## 55. Previous-Year Reuse

Re-check emergency contacts, support hours, insurance, accommodation, staff responsibilities, after-hours procedure, communication tools, medical referral information, and disaster procedures for the current offering.

Do not roll old contacts forward silently.

## 56. Publication Lifecycle

Use:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible blocking review flags:

- `Contact Verification Required`
- `Operational Review Required`
- `External Source Recheck Required`

Unverified contact information blocks publication.

## 57. Change Control

Material changes include emergency contact, support hours, insurance, accommodation emergency route, staff responsibility, safeguarding process, and disaster communication channel.

Review programme pages, pre-arrival guidance, orientation material, emergency references, FAQ, and internal protocol as affected.

## 58. Operational Testing and Scenario Review

Where appropriate, test contact reachability, message distribution, phone routing, staff escalation, and accommodation contact before arrival.

Consider realistic scenarios such as illness at night, hospital visit, lost passport, typhoon disruption, accommodation incident, harassment report, uncontactable student, lost phone, and transport shutdown.

The purpose is to expose unclear responsibility before an incident—not create public promises.

## 59. FAQ Topics

Useful verified questions may include:

- What should I do if I get sick?
- What should I do in an emergency?
- Is insurance required?
- Is someone available at night?
- Will staff come to hospital with me?
- What happens during an earthquake or typhoon?
- Who do I contact about a serious concern?
- What if I lose my passport?
- What support exists if I feel overwhelmed?

## 60. Quality Review Checklist

### Truth
- contacts, support hours, insurance, and medical-support claims are current,
- no absolute safety or unsupported 24/7 claim exists.

### Responsibility
- staff, accommodation, public services, students, buddies, and partner staff have correctly described roles.

### Student Action
- emergency vs routine actions, medical route, insurance responsibility, and support limits are understandable.

### Health / Wellbeing
- medical content is non-diagnostic; mental wellbeing services and medication guidance are verified; accessibility uses a confidential route.

### Disaster / Safeguarding
- official practical guidance is used; serious concerns have a real reporting route; privacy/minor-specific needs are handled appropriately.

### UX / Governance
- urgent actions are easy to find on mobile and accessible; warning hierarchy is proportionate; current owners/sources/status are known.

## 61. Definition of Done

Safety & Support content is complete when responsibility, emergency/routine routes, contacts, insurance, medical-support limits, student independence, wellbeing/accessibility position, disaster guidance, serious-concern routes, privacy, and current-offering verification are all clear and consistent with actual operations.

## 62. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> I understand what support exists.  
> I know who is responsible for what.  
> I know what I should do in an emergency.  
> I know what I am expected to manage myself.  
> I understand the limits of the programme's support.  
> I trust the system because it is specific and realistic.

The goal is not to create the feeling that nothing can go wrong.

The goal is to make students confident that the programme has thought carefully about what happens when something does.
