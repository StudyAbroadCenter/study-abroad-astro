# RSJP Experience System — Programme Page Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs programme-page presentation and decision experience. It does not independently own facts controlled by another domain.

For factual authority:

- programme identity, dates, duration, status, and fee → Programme Offering Domain,
- academics → `13_SYLLABUS_SPEC.md`,
- student interaction → `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`,
- accommodation → `15_ACCOMMODATION_SPEC.md`,
- safety/support → `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`,
- application, eligibility, payment, and refund → `18_BEFORE_YOU_APPLY_SPEC.md`.

Apply `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use:

- `Confirmed`
- `Provisional`
- `Unknown`
- `Not Applicable`

Use `Subject to change` only when a real current plan exists but is not final. Do not use it as a substitute for `Unknown`.

## 1. Purpose

Programme pages are the core decision pages of the RSJP Experience System.

They must help a prospective student:

1. understand what the programme is,
2. imagine participation realistically,
3. judge whether the programme fits their goals and circumstances,
4. verify essential facts,
5. resolve important concerns,
6. trust the information,
7. take the correct next action.

**Emotion from reality. Trust from facts.**

## 2. Non-Negotiable Rules

Programme pages must never:

- invent or infer programme facts,
- turn provisional information into confirmed information,
- fabricate schedules, testimonials, statistics, or student experiences,
- exaggerate guaranteed outcomes,
- imply optional activities are guaranteed,
- imply friendships or cultural outcomes are guaranteed,
- hide restrictions, conditions, or material costs,
- use marketing language to obscure reality.

If a fact is not verified, represent the uncertainty honestly or omit the fact.

## 3. Primary User Outcome

A student should be able to say:

- I know what this programme is.
- I know what makes it different.
- I understand what I would study.
- I can picture the experience realistically.
- I know the dates, duration, and cost.
- I know the key requirements.
- I understand the accommodation position.
- I know the current application status.
- I know what to do next.

## 4. Emotional and Decision Journey

Design for:

1. Interest — this is relevant to me.
2. Understanding — I know what the programme actually is.
3. Imagination — I can picture participation.
4. Value — I understand what I would do and learn.
5. Trust — facts and conditions are clear.
6. Self-assessment — I know whether it fits me.
7. Confidence — my important concerns are addressed.
8. Action — I know what to do next.

The labels do not need to appear visually.

## 5. Recommended Page Architecture

Use only sections supported by real user need and verified information:

1. Hero
2. Essential Programme Snapshot
3. Why This Programme
4. What You Will Experience
5. Academic Experience
6. Typical Day / Experience Pattern, when defensible
7. Student Interaction, when applicable
8. Location and Campus Experience
9. Accommodation
10. What's Included / Not Included
11. Programme Schedule / Journey
12. Eligibility and Requirements
13. Is This Programme Right for You?
14. Safety & Support summary
15. Student Voices, when genuine and permitted
16. FAQ
17. Related / Compare Programmes
18. Final CTA

Do not force every programme into an identical long template.

## 6. Hero

The Hero should quickly answer:

- What is this programme?
- Why might I care?
- What can I do next?

It may contain:

- official programme name,
- concise value statement,
- short support copy,
- one primary CTA,
- one meaningful authentic image,
- current status where useful.

Avoid long institutional introductions, university history, generic slogans, unsupported superlatives, and vague inspiration.

Prefer authentic participation imagery over unrelated destination imagery.

CTA must match the real application state. Never show an active `Apply Now` path when application is unavailable.

## 7. Essential Programme Snapshot

Place decision-critical facts near the top. Typical fields may include:

- location/campus,
- dates,
- duration,
- programme fee,
- application period,
- application status,
- language requirement,
- accommodation summary.

Show the 4–8 facts that matter most for the specific programme. Do not retype changing facts manually when shared structured data exists.

## 8. Why This Programme

Explain the genuine purpose, audience, and differentiation of the programme.

Avoid copy such as `unforgettable experience`, `unique opportunity`, `world-class`, or `perfect for everyone` unless a specific factual basis exists.

If another programme could use the same paragraph unchanged, the copy is probably too generic.

## 9. What You Will Experience

Use real programme elements such as:

- classes,
- cultural sessions,
- campus activities,
- local exploration,
- student interaction,
- workshops,
- academic visits.

Prefer authentic photography, concrete examples, short explanatory copy, and varied editorial layouts.

Do not present past or possible activities as guaranteed current activities. Label examples and variation accurately.

## 10. Academic Experience

Explain in student-friendly language:

- what students study,
- learning structure,
- level where relevant,
- learning goals,
- academic expectations,
- assessment/attendance where relevant.

Provide a programme-level summary and link to the approved web syllabus rather than duplicating the entire syllabus.

## 11. Typical Day / Experience Pattern

Use only when grounded in real programme structure.

A sample day must not be invented for immersion. If days vary, label it clearly, for example:

- A typical weekday may look like this.
- One possible programme day.

Do not publish exact times unless genuinely representative or confirmed.

## 12. Student Interaction

Use `Buddy Programme` only when a formal buddy model actually exists.

Distinguish:

- buddy programme,
- joint learning,
- peer exchange,
- student-led activity,
- club interaction,
- informal social opportunity,
- student support.

Do not promise friendship, one-to-one matching, frequency, or specific participation unless formally guaranteed. Follow `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`.

## 13. Location and Campus Experience

Explain what it means to study and live in the actual location.

Balance campus, daily life, practical context, local culture, and relevant destinations. Do not reduce Kyoto, Osaka, or another location to tourism imagery. Do not reuse identical location copy across campuses.

## 14. Accommodation

Accommodation is a major decision factor. Summarise, where relevant:

- accommodation type/name,
- room arrangement and occupancy,
- location,
- key facilities,
- Wi-Fi/laundry/meals where verified,
- campus relationship,
- important rules/conditions,
- whether provision is guaranteed, conditional, optional, or self-arranged.

Do not imply availability is guaranteed unless it is. Images must not imply a better or more private room than students may actually receive. Label example/typical images accurately.

## 15. What's Included / Not Included

List only confirmed inclusions. Clearly identify major expected costs or responsibilities not covered where relevant.

Possible categories include airfare, meals, local transport, personal expenses, optional activities, and visa-related costs, but do not add categories by assumption.

This section complements rather than replaces official fee information.

## 16. Programme Schedule / Journey

First communicate overall rhythm, then link or expand into detail when available.

Possible stages include:

- arrival/orientation,
- academic phase,
- programme activities,
- final week/completion.

Draft or indicative schedules must be visibly labelled. Mobile must not depend on a wide desktop timetable.

## 17. Eligibility and Requirements

Distinguish clearly:

- required,
- recommended,
- expected preparation,
- important conditions.

State concrete language requirements. Do not hide attendance, participation, or physical requirements until after application.

## 18. Is This Programme Right for You?

Support honest self-selection around:

- academic interest,
- language readiness,
- length,
- cost,
- accommodation,
- willingness to participate,
- independence,
- schedule constraints.

Good recruitment helps unsuitable applicants decide not to apply.

## 19. Safety & Support Summary

Provide a concise overview of real support systems, such as insurance, emergency contact principles, medical support process, orientation, or disaster guidance where verified.

Do not say `completely safe` or `24/7 support` unless formally true and defined. Follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`.

## 20. Student Voices

Use only genuine, approved source material.

Never generate fictional testimonials. Minor editing is acceptable only where publication rules permit it and meaning is preserved. Do not convert authentic student voice into institutional advertising.

Respect consent and privacy.

## 21. FAQ

Use questions grounded in real enquiries, feedback, operational experience, or known confusion.

Answer directly first. Do not hide essential programme facts only inside FAQ accordions. Follow `20_FAQ_SPEC.md`.

## 22. Related Programmes / Comparison

Compare programmes only across meaningful verified dimensions such as duration, season, location, academic focus, fee, accommodation, eligibility, and language requirements.

Do not manufacture comparison dimensions or imply superiority without a defined criterion.

### Cross-Programme Comparability

Comparable facts should use consistent labels, units, date formats, fee presentation, status terminology, and information order where practical.

Consistency must not force genuinely different programme structures into misleading uniformity.

## 23. CTA Strategy

CTA should match the student's decision stage.

Examples:

- early: `Check Dates and Fees`, `See Programme Details`,
- middle: `See Accommodation`, `View Syllabus`, `Check Requirements`,
- late: `Read Application Guidelines`, `Apply Now`.

Status logic must follow `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md` and current application-system reality.

## 24. Mobile Programme Page

Mobile is the primary design environment.

At 390px users should quickly identify:

- programme name,
- status,
- key value,
- essential facts,
- primary next action.

At 320px the experience must remain usable. Avoid hover dependency, tiny controls, oversized sticky UI, and horizontal scrolling for ordinary content.

## 25. Desktop Programme Page

Use desktop space for better composition and comparison through editorial image/text relationships, aligned facts, timelines, and wider photography where useful.

Do not simply enlarge the mobile layout.

## 26. Photography Strategy

Prefer, in order where appropriate:

1. authentic programme participation,
2. student interaction,
3. academic activity,
4. campus life,
5. accommodation,
6. programme location,
7. general destination imagery.

Pair images with nearby factual context when possible: class photo + academic structure, accommodation photo + room facts, activity photo + real explanation.

## 27. Content Density and Page Length

Avoid brochure walls, policy walls, and endless card grids.

Alternate visual moments, factual summaries, explanation, and detail.

Long pages are acceptable when length supports real decisions. Do not shorten important facts for minimal aesthetics or lengthen pages with filler.

## 28. Accessibility

Support semantic headings, keyboard navigation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, meaningful links, alt text, reduced motion, and textual status.

Do not rely on colour, hover, animation, or text embedded in images.

## 29. Performance

Use responsive images, appropriate compression, lazy loading where appropriate, explicit image dimensions/aspect ratios, and minimal client-side JavaScript.

Do not trade mobile performance for decorative immersion.

## 30. SEO

Each programme page should be independently understandable from search results with accurate title, factual description, canonical URL where appropriate, meaningful Open Graph image, and logical headings.

Do not create misleading SEO copy.

## 31. Data Model Relationship

Programme pages may draw from structured concepts such as:

- identity,
- offering,
- dates,
- fee,
- status,
- academics,
- experiences,
- student interaction,
- accommodation,
- location,
- eligibility,
- schedule,
- safety/support,
- FAQ,
- application.

This is conceptual. Do not create fields merely because the list exists.

## 32. Fact Ownership and Verification Metadata

Where operationally practical, decision-critical facts should carry internal metadata such as source, owner, last verified date, confirmation status, and offering applicability.

Do not invent metadata. Unknown ownership is itself a governance issue.

## 33. Programme-Specific Flexibility

Consistency means shared trust, data, accessibility, CTA, and design rules—not identical page layouts.

Programmes may differ through photography, section emphasis, academics, location, duration, experience, accommodation, and audience.

## 34. Unknown and Unconfirmed Information

Design unknown states intentionally.

Use honest language such as `Dates to be confirmed` or `Accommodation details will be announced when confirmed` only when those statements are true.

Avoid blank labels, fake dates, zeroes, placeholder values, and previous-year values presented as current.

## 35. Version-Specific Information

Stable programme identity may persist. Offering-specific facts must be re-verified, including dates, fee, accommodation, schedule, application period, capacity, and status.

## 36. Publication Readiness

A polished page is not publishable merely because design is complete.

At minimum determine the state of:

- programme identity,
- current offering status,
- dates,
- fee,
- eligibility,
- application action,
- accommodation.

### Publication Workflow

Use the canonical lifecycle:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible review flags include:

- `Cross-Programme Consistency Check Required`
- `Operational Review Required`
- `Translation Review Required`

## 37. Review Checklist

Before publication verify:

### Truth
- all programme facts are verified or honestly marked,
- no old-year values are silently reused,
- no testimonial/statistic/activity is fabricated,
- decision-critical facts have an authoritative domain/source.

### Student Decision
- dates, fee, status, eligibility, accommodation, and next action are easy to find,
- comparable facts are presented consistently.

### Experience and Academic Value
- students can imagine participation from real programme elements,
- academic value is understandable,
- syllabus access exists where appropriate.

### Trust
- limitations and inclusions/exclusions are visible,
- uncertainty is represented honestly.

### UX / UI
- 390px and 320px work,
- content is scannable,
- CTA matches status,
- important information is not hidden,
- layout avoids repetitive generic card treatment.

### Accessibility / Performance
- heading/focus/link/status behaviour is accessible,
- images are responsive,
- unnecessary JavaScript is avoided,
- layout is stable during loading.

## 38. Definition of Done

A programme page is complete when it is understandable, accurate, decision-useful, academically clear, honest about requirements/accommodation, mobile-strong, accessible, and aligned with real CTA/application status.

No unverified information may be presented as fact.

## 39. Final Standard

A successful page should leave the student thinking:

> I understand what this programme really is.  
> I can imagine myself taking part.  
> I know the important facts.  
> I know whether it suits me.  
> I trust the information.  
> I know what to do next.

The page should create excitement without manufacturing it.

The programme itself should provide the reason to apply.
