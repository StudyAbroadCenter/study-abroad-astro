# RSJP Experience System — Accommodation Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs the **Accommodation Domain**.

Current accommodation facts should be supported by current booking, contract, provider confirmation, or another approved accommodation source. Programme Page, Comparison, Before You Apply, Before You Arrive, and FAQ are derivative views for accommodation facts.

Follow `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

Accommodation strongly affects trust, comfort, cost, daily routine, independence, safety perception, satisfaction, and willingness to apply.

Accommodation information must be accurate, current, understandable, operationally deliverable, visually useful, and honest about limitations.

**Comfort from clarity. Trust from accuracy.**

## 2. Accommodation Is Not Decoration

Students need to understand:

- where they may stay,
- room type and occupancy,
- privacy/shared facilities,
- facilities and meals,
- campus access,
- rules,
- inclusions/exclusions,
- guarantee/availability status,
- alternatives if the standard option is unavailable.

Do not treat accommodation as lifestyle imagery only.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Accuracy Rules

Never invent or infer:

- property name,
- room type or layout,
- occupancy,
- bathroom/kitchen arrangement,
- distance/commute,
- Wi-Fi/laundry/meals/bedding/amenities,
- cost,
- check-in/out,
- accessibility,
- curfew/rules,
- gender allocation,
- availability or guarantee status.

Previous-year accommodation is reference material only until re-verified.

## 4. Accommodation Models

### Programme-Provided Accommodation
Arranged as part of the programme. This does not automatically mean guaranteed, included, private, or a fixed property.

### Conditional Programme Accommodation
Available only when defined conditions are met, such as property capacity, group model, accompanying staff, or allocation constraints.

### Partner / Group-Specific Accommodation
Available only to a particular partner/group. Do not imply availability to independent applicants.

### External Accommodation
Arranged through an external hotel/residence/provider. Clarify who books, who pays, and whether it is included.

### Self-Arranged Accommodation
Students arrange accommodation independently. Explain responsibilities, any programme conditions, and whether fees change only if that rule is verified.

## 5. Guarantee Status

Use the canonical guarantee model:

- `Guaranteed`
- `Conditional`
- `Optional`
- `Not Provided`
- `Unknown`

`Subject to availability` is a condition, not a guarantee state.

`Self-arranged` describes the delivery model, not the guarantee state.

Do not use vague `Accommodation available` wording when provision is conditional.

## 6. Programme Page Summary

A concise summary should let students answer:

- Is accommodation provided?
- Is it included?
- What type is it?
- Is it guaranteed?
- Is it shared?
- How does it relate to campus?

## 7. Complete Accommodation Information

Where relevant include:

- accommodation name/type,
- provider/operator where useful,
- location,
- room type,
- occupancy,
- bed arrangement,
- bathroom,
- kitchen,
- Wi-Fi,
- laundry,
- meals,
- bedding/linen,
- room/common facilities,
- campus access,
- check-in/out,
- rules,
- accessibility,
- cost/inclusion,
- availability conditions,
- alternative arrangements.

Do not show empty fields merely to complete a template.

## 8. Room Type and Occupancy

Use precise terms: single room, twin room, shared room, dormitory-style, apartment-style, etc.

Do not call a private bedroom in a shared unit `single` without explaining the shared unit.

State meaningful occupancy clearly, e.g. `2 students per twin room` or `up to 4 students per room`. Do not hide high occupancy behind `shared accommodation`.

## 9. Roommate Allocation

Where relevant explain the real allocation principle: programme cohort, group allocation, random allocation, gender-based policy, etc.

Do not promise a specific roommate, nationality mix, age match, or compatibility.

## 10. Gender and Individual Needs

Use only the actual approved room-assignment policy and describe it sensitively.

Where individual accommodation needs require discussion, provide a confidential route rather than public disclosure.

## 11. Privacy / Bathroom / Kitchen

Distinguish accurately:

- private vs shared bedroom,
- en-suite/private/shared/communal bathroom,
- full kitchen/kitchenette/microwave/refrigerator/no cooking facilities,
- shared common spaces.

Do not imply private facilities through imagery when the arrangement is shared.

## 12. Meals

State whether breakfast or other meals are included, a meal plan exists, students self-cater, or meals are not provided.

Do not use `meals included` without defining scope.

## 13. Laundry

State actual facilities where known: in-room washer, shared laundry, coin laundry, or external commercial laundry.

Do not describe an unrelated nearby service as an in-property facility.

## 14. Wi-Fi / Internet

State whether internet is included/property Wi-Fi/shared/limited/not guaranteed.

Do not promise `high-speed Wi-Fi` or reliability without evidence.

## 15. Bedding / Cleaning / Furnishings

Clarify verified provisions such as bedding, sheets, towels, cleaning, desk/chair/storage, climate control, refrigerator, etc.

Do not imply hotel-level service in a residence unless it exists.

## 16. Location and Commute

Explain the accommodation's practical relationship to campus and relevant daily services.

Where possible describe:

- typical mode,
- approximate travel time,
- transfers,
- walking component,
- who pays transport costs.

Do not present a timetable-perfect minimum as a guaranteed door-to-door commute. Verify route claims.

## 17. Check-In / Check-Out

Explain confirmed:

- date/time window,
- reception/key procedure,
- late-arrival restrictions,
- check-out time,
- key return,
- room condition expectations,
- luggage information where verified.

Do not reuse last year's instructions without confirmation.

## 18. Early Arrival / Late Departure

Clearly distinguish programme accommodation dates from independently arranged extensions.

Do not imply early check-in, extended stay, or baggage storage is possible unless confirmed.

## 19. Rules

Publish material rules where useful, for example quiet hours, visitors, smoking, alcohol, cooking, waste separation, room access, shared-space use, and keys.

Do not invent a curfew. Distinguish building access restrictions from behavioural curfew.

Detailed operational rules may live in a check-in/pre-arrival guide rather than the recruitment page.

## 20. Damage / Charges / Deposits

If students may be responsible for damage, lost keys, excessive cleaning, or deposits, explain material conditions at the appropriate stage.

Do not invent charge amounts or use `deposit` for a non-refundable fee.

## 21. Cost

When accommodation cost is separate, state:

- amount,
- currency,
- pricing basis,
- number of nights,
- relevant taxes/inclusions where verified.

Where practical show the total rather than forcing students to calculate it.

If accommodation is included in the programme fee, say so clearly.

## 22. Refund / Cancellation

Accommodation cancellation may differ from programme cancellation. Do not merge policies unless they are truly identical.

Publish confirmed deadlines, non-refundable amounts, and provider-specific conditions where relevant.

## 23. Changes and Alternatives

Accommodation may change because of capacity, availability, operational issues, programme size, or provider changes.

If the programme reserves the right to change property, explain that principle honestly.

Do not promise alternative equivalence in location, room type, facilities, commute, or price unless genuinely guaranteed.

## 24. Property Images

Prefer actual current-enough images of rooms, common areas, exterior, bathroom/kitchen where useful, and practical context.

Avoid generic stock imagery.

If the photo may not represent the exact assigned room, label it accurately (`Example room`, `Typical room type`, etc.) only when that remains truthful.

Verify old imagery after renovations/reconfiguration.

## 25. Floor Plans

Use only accurate, approved floor plans. Never draw approximate plans from memory.

## 26. Accessibility

Do not state `accessible` unless verified.

Accessibility may depend on step-free access, lift, bathroom, dimensions, route to campus, and evacuation considerations.

Avoid oversimplified binary claims where suitability depends on the individual. Provide a confidential contact route.

## 27. Health / Dietary / Religious Needs

Do not promise specific accommodation for medical, health, dietary, allergy, religious, or cultural needs before feasibility is confirmed.

Shared kitchens may carry cross-contact risk. Do not guarantee allergen-free environments without evidence.

Use respectful confidential consultation routes where appropriate.

## 28. Safety Information

Accommodation content may explain verified building access, emergency exits, property contact, programme emergency route, or local procedures.

Do not claim `completely safe`, `crime-free`, `always supervised`, or `high security`.

Distinguish property contact, programme emergency contact, university contact, and public emergency services.

## 29. Overnight Staff / Front Desk

Do not imply 24-hour staffing unless verified. State limited reception hours when operationally important.

## 30. Student Independence

Present accommodation realistically. Students may need to commute, cook, shop, do laundry, share space, follow rules, and manage time independently.

A residence should not be marketed as a fully serviced hotel unless it is one.

## 31. Residence vs Hotel Expectations

Clarify differences in housekeeping, towels, reception, room service, amenities, and cleaning where they affect expectations.

## 32. Shared Living

Pre-arrival guidance may explain practical expectations around noise, cleaning, shared facilities, and respect for others.

Do not romanticise shared rooms.

## 33. Personal Belongings / Luggage / Mail

Only publish verified information about storage, packages/mail, and forwarding. Do not imply the programme insures personal property or transports luggage unless true.

## 34. Legal / Administrative Requirements

Use current official information for any legal accommodation/registration procedure. Do not provide immigration/registration advice from assumption.

## 35. Minors

If minors are accepted, accommodation may require additional safeguards or restrictions. These must be formally verified; adult rules do not automatically apply.

## 36. Accompanying Staff and Group Accommodation

Where group accommodation depends on accompanying staff, state the condition accurately where relevant.

Do not imply accompanying staff provide hotel management or 24-hour supervision unless formally assigned.

Distinguish student/staff room arrangements without publishing unnecessary commercial detail.

## 37. Independent Applicants

If independent applicants have different accommodation eligibility or conditions from partner groups, make the difference clear before application.

## 38. Capacity and Allocation Timing

Accommodation capacity may constrain programme capacity. Do not sell places beyond deliverable accommodation capacity where accommodation is required/guaranteed.

Do not publish exact room/property assignment before allocation is confirmed.

## 39. Data Model

Conceptual fields may include:

- accommodation ID/name/type/provider,
- offering applicability,
- guarantee state,
- delivery model,
- room type/occupancy,
- bathroom/kitchen,
- Wi-Fi/laundry/meals/facilities,
- location/commute,
- transport-cost responsibility,
- check-in/out,
- rules/accessibility,
- cost/currency/pricing basis,
- inclusion status,
- availability,
- image references,
- source/owner/last verified date.

Do not create unused fields solely because this list exists.

## 40. Source of Truth and Verification

Preferred evidence includes current property contract, booking confirmation, provider confirmation, approved internal arrangement, and verified official property information.

When contractual information differs from marketing copy, do not treat provider marketing copy as the authority.

Track owner, source, verification date, offering, and confirmation status where practical.

## 41. Previous-Year Reuse

Re-verify property, dates, nights, price, room type, occupancy, availability, rules, facilities, commute, check-in/out, and cancellation terms.

Previous-year accommodation is not current by default.

## 42. Publication Lifecycle

Use:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible blocking review flags:

- `Provider Confirmation Required`
- `Operational Review Required`

A beautiful page is not publishable while key accommodation facts remain unconfirmed.

## 43. Change Control

Material changes include property, occupancy, cost, commute, inclusion, guarantee state, and significant facility changes.

Review all affected Programme Page, Comparison, Before You Apply, Before You Arrive, and FAQ content.

## 44. Programme Comparison

Compare only meaningful verified dimensions such as provided/self-arranged, room type, occupancy, included/separate cost, campus access, and guarantee/condition.

Do not use simplistic accommodation quality scores.

## 45. FAQ Topics

Useful verified questions may include:

- Is accommodation included?
- Is it guaranteed?
- Will I have my own room?
- Who will I share with?
- Can I choose my roommate?
- Is Wi-Fi/laundry/cooking available?
- Are meals included?
- How do I get to campus?
- Can I arrive early or stay longer?
- What if the standard option is full?

A dedicated page may use:

1. Overview
2. Guarantee/availability
3. Room arrangement
4. Gallery
5. Facilities
6. Daily living
7. Campus access
8. Cost/inclusions
9. Check-in/out
10. Important rules
11. Accessibility/individual needs
12. FAQ
13. Programme-specific differences

Use only needed sections.

## 47. UI / Mobile / Accessibility / Performance

Prioritise practical understanding through authentic photos, fact summaries, clear room/facility labels, commute explanation, and visible status.

Avoid hotel-booking star ratings, luxury language, excessive lifestyle imagery, or unlabeled amenity icons.

At 390px quickly show type, guarantee status, occupancy, cost/inclusion, campus relationship, and key facilities. At 320px essential content must remain usable.

Use semantic headings, meaningful alt text, accessible galleries, text alternatives for maps/floor plans where needed, and status text beyond colour.

Use responsive/compressed/lazy images and restrained gallery JavaScript.

## 48. Tone

Practical, reassuring, honest, warm, and precise.

Avoid luxury marketing, vague reassurance, fear-based language, and exaggerated comfort claims.

## 49. Quality Review Checklist

### Truth
- property/type, occupancy, guarantee, price, nights, facilities, images, commute, and current-offering applicability are verified.

### Student Decision
- inclusion, guarantee, sharing, daily living, campus access, and limitations are clear.

### Cost
- total cost where separate, currency, inclusions/exclusions, and cancellation/refund conditions are accurate.

### Operations
- advertised accommodation can actually be delivered; provider confirmation/capacity/fallback are understood.

### Privacy / Inclusion
- assignment language is sensitive; accessibility claims verified; confidential route exists for individual needs.

### Safety
- security/support descriptions are factual; emergency responsibility is correctly assigned; no false 24-hour promise.

### UX / Governance
- mobile works; photos aid understanding; current owner/source/verification state is known where practical.

## 50. Definition of Done

Accommodation content is complete when the model, guarantee state, room type/occupancy, facilities, campus access, cost/inclusion, rules/limitations, accessibility position, imagery, and operational delivery are accurately represented for the current offering.

## 51. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> I understand where I may stay.  
> I understand the room arrangement.  
> I know what is included and what I manage myself.  
> I know how I will get to campus.  
> I know what is guaranteed and what depends on conditions.  
> I know the important rules and limitations.  
> I trust the information because it is practical and specific.

Accommodation should not be sold as a dream. It should be presented as a real place where students can understand how they will live.
