# RSJP Experience System — Content and Language Specification

## Purpose

Content exists to help prospective students make informed decisions.

The desired path is:

interest → understanding → trust → decision → action.

Content is not an archive of everything the university knows.

## Reader

Write primarily for prospective international university students.

Many may:

- use English as a second/additional language,
- know little about Japanese universities,
- compare several programmes,
- read on mobile,
- worry about cost, accommodation, language ability, safety, and procedure.

## Accuracy

Accuracy has priority over persuasion.

Never:

- invent facts,
- estimate official information without clearly labelling it,
- hide important conditions,
- describe unconfirmed plans as confirmed,
- exaggerate outcomes.

## Information Order

Generally prefer:

1. why the programme matters,
2. what students experience,
3. essential facts,
4. requirements,
5. practical details,
6. administrative detail.

## Voice

Be:

- warm,
- clear,
- confident,
- informed,
- human,
- credible,
- international.

Avoid:

- exaggerated,
- bureaucratic,
- overly academic,
- childish,
- aggressively promotional language.

## Confidence Without Hype

Use concrete details instead of unsupported adjectives.

Specificity creates credibility.

## Scannability

Important information should survive scanning.

Use:

- descriptive headings,
- short paragraphs,
- structured facts,
- meaningful links,
- concise labels,
- clear CTAs.

## Sentence and Paragraph Length

Prefer short to medium sentences.

One paragraph should normally communicate one central idea.

Avoid mobile walls of text.

## Vocabulary

Prefer familiar, precise words.

Use formal terminology only when it adds accuracy.

## Japanese

Japanese should be professional, approachable, concise, and understandable.

Avoid unnecessary administrative expressions when simpler Japanese is accurate.

## English

Use clear international English.

Avoid culture-specific slang and unnecessary idiom.

Do not deliberately make the language childish; the audience is university-level.

## Translation

Translation is not sentence substitution.

Process:

1. identify facts,
2. identify purpose,
3. identify what the student needs,
4. rewrite naturally,
5. verify factual equivalence.

Japanese sentence structure does not need to survive in English.

## Factual Equivalence

Language versions may differ in:

- order,
- examples,
- explanation length,
- cultural context.

They must not differ in:

- dates,
- fee,
- eligibility,
- deadline,
- capacity,
- accommodation conditions,
- cancellation conditions,
- status.

## Source of Truth

Language versions are presentation layers, not independent factual authorities.

For programme-facing facts, follow `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md` and the relevant authoritative domain.

If Japanese is the operational drafting language for a piece of editorial copy, it may be the translation reference for meaning, but it must not override an authoritative domain fact.

Shared structured programme data should reduce language-level duplication for changing facts.

## Headlines

Prefer meaning before creativity.

Strong headings tell users what the section gives them.

Avoid empty slogans as primary information.

## Hero Copy

Keep concise.

Communicate:

- identity/value,
- essential context,
- primary action.

Do not put the whole programme description in the hero.

## CTA Language

Prefer:

- Apply Now
- View Programme
- Compare Programmes
- Check Dates and Fees
- See Accommodation
- Read Application Guidelines
- Contact Us

Avoid vague `Click Here`.

## Programme Descriptions

Answer:

- What is it?
- Who is it for?
- What makes it different?
- What will I do?
- Why might I choose it?

Do not begin with organisational history unless it matters to the decision.

## Programme Facts

Present consistently:

- dates,
- duration,
- campus/location,
- fee,
- capacity,
- application period,
- accommodation,
- language level,
- status.

## Fees

State:

- amount,
- currency,
- included items,
- important exclusions,
- relevant refund/cancellation information.

Do not make users reconstruct essential cost from scattered pages.

## Dates

Avoid ambiguous numeric-only English dates.

Adopt one project date format once approved and use it consistently.

Do not invent a format in code if the project already defines one.

## Accommodation

Answer practical questions:

- where,
- room arrangement,
- key facilities,
- relation to campus,
- important conditions.

Do not write accommodation as pure lifestyle promotion.

## Eligibility

Separate:

- required,
- recommended,
- expected preparation.

Do not make recommendations appear mandatory.

## Language Requirements

Be concrete.

If a script or skill is required, state it directly.

## Application Instructions

Make sequence clear:

1. what to prepare,
2. what to submit,
3. when,
4. what happens next.

Avoid internal workflow terminology.

## Programme Status

Use consistent approved labels.

Recommended English concepts:

- Applications Open
- Applications Closed
- Coming Soon
- Full
- Cancelled

Final labels should be centralised rather than duplicated arbitrarily.

## FAQ

Questions should resemble actual applicant questions.

Answer directly first, then explain.

## Cultural Explanation

Explain context without exoticising Japan.

Avoid mystery, timelessness, or spectacle framing.

## Location Content

Kyoto content should balance heritage and contemporary student life.

Other locations should have their own voice; do not reuse Kyoto language automatically.

## Student Stories

Keep specific and human.

Do not edit authentic student voices until they sound like corporate advertising.

## Testimonials

Use only with appropriate context and permission.

Do not use testimonials to support claims they cannot substantiate.

## Statistics

Use only accurate, current-enough, relevant, contextualised statistics.

Never invent metrics.

## Institutional Terminology

Use official terminology where necessary.

Explain difficult terms in plain language.

Avoid exposing internal acronyms without purpose.

## Inclusive Language

Do not unnecessarily assume:

- gender,
- nationality,
- family structure,
- ability,
- cultural background.

## Accessibility

Link text, headings, labels, and instructions must make sense without relying only on colour or position.

## Error Content

Explain:

- what went wrong,
- what to do next.

Avoid technical implementation language.

## Empty Content

If information is unavailable, say so clearly.

Do not use placeholder facts that could be mistaken for confirmed information.

## SEO

Search optimisation must not damage clarity or natural language.

No keyword stuffing.

## Avoiding Generic AI Writing

Avoid default formulas such as:

- Embark on a journey...
- Immerse yourself...
- Nestled in the heart of...
- Discover the perfect blend...
- Whether you're...
- More than just...
- It's not just X — it's Y.

These are not grammatically forbidden; they simply must not become the default voice.

## Human Quality

Write as people who understand the programme.

Useful detail beats filler.

## Progressive Disclosure

Decision-critical information first.

Policies and exceptions later.

## Content Review

Check:

- accuracy,
- clarity,
- relevance,
- trust,
- consistency,
- natural target-language writing,
- clear action.

## Priority

1. Accuracy
2. Student understanding
3. Trust
4. Accessibility
5. Clarity
6. Actionability
7. Brand voice
8. Persuasion
9. Search optimisation
10. Literary style
