# RSJP Experience System — Syllabus Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs the **Academic Domain**. Academic facts are authoritative only when supported by the current approved syllabus, academic owner, or another approved academic source.

It does not own programme dates/fees, accommodation, application rules, or operational safety.

Apply `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

The syllabus is the authoritative academic explanation of a course or structured academic component.

It should help students and partner universities understand:

- what students study,
- intended learning outcomes,
- course level and prerequisites,
- language of instruction,
- teaching structure,
- contact hours and workload,
- assessment/grading,
- attendance,
- materials,
- academic policies,
- schedule status,
- credit/recognition where formally applicable.

**Academic clarity without institutional obscurity.**

## 2. Relationship to the Programme Page

The programme page explains overall value, experience, dates, fee, accommodation, and application.

The syllabus explains academic substance, expectations, learning, workload, and assessment.

Do not duplicate an entire programme page inside the syllabus or force unrelated operational information into it.

## 3. Student-First Academic Communication

Write for international university students who may not understand Japanese university terminology, class-period conventions, grading systems, or internal faculty structures.

Use official terminology where required, but explain it in plain language.

## 4. Non-Negotiable Accuracy Rules

Never invent or infer:

- course titles/codes,
- learning outcomes,
- credits,
- contact hours,
- assessment methods/weights,
- grading boundaries,
- attendance rules,
- prerequisites,
- level equivalencies,
- instructor names,
- textbooks,
- schedules,
- academic policies.

Do not roll a previous-year syllabus forward without re-verification.

## 5. Publication Lifecycle

Use:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible blocking review flags:

- `Academic Approval Required`
- `Translation Review Required`

Only current approved/published academic information should be treated as official.

## 6. Syllabus Identity

Where real and useful, record:

- syllabus ID/course code,
- official course title,
- approved short title,
- programme/offering,
- term/year,
- language of instruction,
- campus/location,
- version,
- last verified date.

Do not invent a course code simply to complete the template.

## 7. Course vs Programme

A programme may contain multiple academic components such as Japanese language, Japan Studies, lectures, workshops, field-based learning, or other classes.

Do not automatically treat the whole programme as one course.

## 8. Standard Syllabus Sections

Use where applicable:

1. Course Identity
2. Course Overview
3. Learning Outcomes
4. Intended Level / Prerequisites
5. Language of Instruction
6. Contact Hours
7. Expected Student Workload
8. Teaching and Learning Methods
9. Course Structure / Topics
10. Assessment
11. Grading
12. Attendance and Participation
13. Materials
14. Academic Integrity
15. Accessibility / Learning Support
16. Schedule
17. Changes to the Course
18. Academic Credit / Recognition, where formally applicable
19. Responsible Academic Unit / Contact, where useful
20. Version / Approval Information

Do not show empty sections merely for symmetry.

## 9. Course Overview

Explain the academic purpose, what students do, how the course fits the programme, and who it is intended for.

Avoid institutional mission statements, generic promotion, and unnecessary history.

## 10. Learning Outcomes

Outcomes should describe what students should reasonably be able to demonstrate.

Prefer observable verbs such as identify, explain, apply, compare, interpret, communicate, produce, analyse, or reflect.

Do not publish vague claims such as `understand Japan deeply` unless operationally defined.

## 11. Learning Outcome Integrity

Every outcome should be:

- taught,
- achievable in the actual course duration,
- supported by course activity,
- assessable where formal assessment applies.

Do not add impressive-sounding outcomes that the course cannot support.

## 12. Outcome–Assessment Alignment

Students and reviewers should be able to understand what students are expected to learn and how they will demonstrate it.

Do not assess outcomes that were not taught or publish outcomes with no plausible evidence of achievement.

## 13. Intended Level

Use the programme's approved terminology, for example beginner, elementary, intermediate, advanced, mixed, or placement-based.

Do not invent JLPT, CEFR, JF Standard, ACTFL, or other equivalencies.

## 14. Prerequisites

Separate:

- mandatory prerequisites,
- recommended preparation,
- placement requirements.

Do not make recommendations appear mandatory.

## 15. Placement

If placement is used, explain timing, purpose, whether level may change, and whether placement is provisional.

Do not imply free student choice when academic placement determines level.

## 16. Language of Instruction

State the actual teaching language(s) where useful, including mixed models such as Japanese with English support when that is true.

## 17. Contact Hours

Make academic load unambiguous.

Where practical state:

- number of sessions,
- actual minutes per session,
- total instructional minutes,
- clock hours.

Avoid an unexplained `30 hours` when `hour` could mean 45, 50, 60, or 90 minutes.

## 18. Clock Hours vs Class Periods

Example structure:

- 10 sessions,
- 90 minutes per session,
- 900 instructional minutes,
- 15 clock hours.

Do not label 10 × 90-minute sessions as 10 hours.

## 19. Credits

Never state or imply academic credits unless formally approved.

If official Ritsumeikan credit exists, state the approved value appropriately. If not, do not create an equivalent value.

## 20. Home-University Credit Transfer

Do not guarantee recognition by the student's home institution.

Where relevant state that credit recognition is determined by the home institution.

Academic documentation may support review but cannot guarantee external credit.

## 21. Student Workload

Where known, distinguish contact time from homework, preparation, reading, projects, independent study, and field-based work.

Do not fabricate workload estimates.

## 22. Teaching and Learning Methods

List only methods actually used, such as language practice, lecture, discussion, pair/group work, presentations, projects, guided observation, field learning, or reflection.

## 23. Experiential and Cultural Learning

When cultural or field activities have academic purpose, explain that purpose.

Distinguish academic activity from optional social activity, tourism, or free time. Do not label every excursion `academic` for marketing value.

## 24. Course Topics

Use actual planned topics in concise student-readable language.

If content varies by level, say so rather than inventing one universal topic sequence.

## 25. Schedule

Use the format that matches the real course:

- session-by-session,
- weekly,
- unit-based,
- topic-based.

Do not force a weekly model onto an intensive short course.

## 26. Draft / Variable Schedules

Label draft or indicative schedules clearly.

`Subject to change` is not permission to publish inaccurate information. Changes must remain consistent with the academic substance and learning outcomes.

## 27. Assessment Overview

Where applicable, state:

- assessment type,
- purpose,
- timing,
- weight,
- submission method,
- individual/group nature,
- relationship to completion.

Do not hide major assessed work in separate administrative documents.

## 28. Assessment Types

Only list assessment actually used, for example quizzes, tests, oral assessment, presentations, assignments, projects, participation, portfolio, or final assessment.

## 29. Assessment Weights

If percentages are used, they must be mathematically coherent and normally total 100%.

If another system is used, explain it rather than forcing percentage weights.

Never invent weights to complete the template.

## 30. Participation as Assessment

If participation is graded, define what it means.

Do not grade personality or extroversion. Explain observable expectations such as preparation, class tasks, pair/group work, or contribution.

## 31. Attendance

State the approved rule where attendance affects completion, certificate eligibility, assessment, or academic standing.

Do not invent minimum percentages.

## 32. Absence and Late Arrival

Explain actual rules where relevant, including recording, make-up work, excessive absence, and late arrival.

Avoid punitive wording beyond real policy.

## 33. Grading

Explain the actual grading model: letter grade, numerical score, pass/fail, completion, rubric, or another approved system.

Do not convert between systems without approval.

## 34. Grade Boundaries

Publish boundaries only if they are the approved rule. Do not assume common 90/80/70 thresholds.

## 35. Completion vs Grade

A completion certificate is not automatically academic credit or a transcript. State the distinction clearly.

## 36. Transcript / Academic Record

If issued, explain the document, contents, typical availability, and official status only where verified.

Do not promise unconfirmed issue dates.

## 37. Materials

State required textbook/workbook/printed/digital materials where confirmed and whether they are included, loaned, purchased, or optional.

Do not publish a textbook/edition before confirmation.

## 38. Devices and Technology

If students need a laptop, tablet, smartphone, software, or internet access, say so.

Do not assume unrestricted access to paid software or app stores.

## 39. Academic Integrity

Use the approved applicable rules for plagiarism, cheating, collaboration, translation tools, and generative AI.

Do not invent policy.

## 40. Generative AI

If AI use is permitted, restricted, or prohibited, state the real course rule. Distinguish uses where appropriate.

Do not impose one blanket rule across all courses without approval.

## 41. Accessibility and Learning Support

Explain verified routes for reasonable support where appropriate.

Do not promise a specific accommodation before feasibility/process is confirmed. Avoid unnecessary public disclosure of sensitive information.

## 42. Physical / Field Requirements

If activities require meaningful walking, travel, physical participation, or environmental exposure, describe the expectation factually before enrolment.

## 43. Safety in Academic Activities

Summarise academically relevant expectations and link to formal Safety & Support guidance where needed. Do not make blanket safety guarantees.

## 44. Instructor Information

Include only confirmed and appropriate details such as name, role, academic area, or approved contact route.

If assignment is not final, say `Instructor to be confirmed` rather than copying a previous instructor.

## 45. Responsible Academic Unit

Use verified official names only where they help the student or partner institution. Do not expose irrelevant internal complexity.

## 46. Communication Expectations

State required academic platforms/channels only when confirmed. Separate academic communication from emergency contact.

## 47. Academic Policies

Link or summarise only policies that actually apply. Do not flood short-course syllabi with unrelated university regulation.

## 48. Course Changes

Reasonable changes may occur because of placement, academic progression, instructor judgement, operational constraints, or field conditions.

Changes must not undermine published outcomes or materially misrepresent the course students joined.

## 49. Cancelled / Replaced Sessions

Replacement academic content should remain appropriate where feasible. Do not silently remove advertised academic substance without review.

## 50. Bilingual / Multilingual Syllabi

Language versions may differ in sentence structure and explanation but must not differ in:

- contact hours,
- outcomes,
- assessment,
- grading,
- attendance,
- prerequisites,
- schedule status,
- credits.

## 51. Academic-Term Translation

Translate meaning, not terminology mechanically. Where no clean equivalent exists, keep the official term and explain it.

Do not create false equivalencies.

## 52. Web-First Syllabus

Prefer a structured web syllabus for mobile readability, accessibility, searchability, consistency, and maintainability.

PDF may remain an export/official document where required, but should not be the only readable format unless policy requires it.

## 53. PDF Export

Where a PDF exists, reconcile it with the same approved academic source as the web version. Do not maintain two unrelated syllabi that can drift.

## 54. Printing

Printable syllabi must remain understandable without hover, animation, hidden accordions, or colour-only meaning.

## 55. Mobile Presentation

At 390px students should quickly identify course title, level, learning purpose, contact hours, assessment overview, and key requirements.

At 320px remain usable. Do not force wide desktop tables.

## 56. Tables

Use tables only when row/column relationships improve understanding, such as assessment breakdown, schedule, or grade scale.

Stack or simplify on mobile where clearer. Never shrink text to unreadability.

## 57. Accessibility

Use semantic headings, logical reading order, keyboard access, readable contrast, meaningful links, accessible tables, and text equivalents.

## 58. Data Model Relationship

Conceptual structured fields may include syllabus identity, course identity, programme/offering relationship, level, language, prerequisites, contact minutes, outcomes, methods, assessment, grading, attendance, materials, schedule, policies, and version metadata.

Do not create unused fields solely because they are listed.

## 59. Shared Facts

Facts shared with programme pages should come from one authoritative/shared source where practical.

## 60. Verification Metadata

Where practical track academic owner, source document, reviewer, last verified date, approval status, and applicable offering.

Do not invent metadata.

## 61. Previous-Year Reuse

A previous syllabus is a reference, not a current syllabus. Re-verify title, instructor, contact hours, outcomes, materials, assessment, grading, attendance, schedule, and policies.

## 62. Cross-Programme Consistency

Equivalent academic concepts should use consistent terminology and units where possible without forcing false uniformity.

## 63. External Academic Review

Where appropriate provide enough information for partner institutions to evaluate level, outcomes, contact hours, assessment, workload, and content.

Do not add invented credit equivalencies to make review easier.

## 64. Change Control and Versioning

Material changes include learning outcomes, contact hours, assessment weights, grading, attendance, mandatory materials, and prerequisites.

Published syllabi should support clear offering/version identification where practical. Archived versions must be clearly non-current.

## 65. Quality Review

Before publication verify:

### Academic Truth
- facts, outcomes, hours, credits, assessment, attendance, and materials are current and approved.

### Student Understanding
- level, expectations, workload where known, and assessment are understandable.

### Alignment
- outcomes match teaching and assessment; schedule supports the stated course; programme page describes the same academic reality.

### International Clarity
- Japanese conventions are explained; clock hours are clear; external credit is not overpromised.

### UX / Accessibility
- mobile presentation works; tables are responsive/accessible; web format is usable without PDF.

### Governance
- current version and offering are identifiable; owner/reviewer is known where required; previous-year data was re-verified.

## 66. Definition of Done

A syllabus is complete when academic facts are verified, outcomes defensible, contact hours unambiguous, level/prerequisites clear, assessment/grading/attendance accurate, credit language honest, materials current, schedule status clear, mobile/accessibility strong, multilingual facts equivalent, and publication/version status clear.

## 67. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> I understand what I will study.  
> I understand what is expected of me.  
> I know how much academic work is involved.  
> I understand how I will be assessed.  
> I know what rules matter.  
> I trust that this is current and accurate.

A partner institution should be able to understand the academic substance without guessing how Japanese institutional conventions translate into its own system.

The syllabus should be academically rigorous, internationally legible, and operationally honest.
