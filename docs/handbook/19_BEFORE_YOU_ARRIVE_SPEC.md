# RSJP Experience System — Before You Arrive Specification

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Formal handbook specification

## Cross-Spec Governance

This specification governs the **Pre-Arrival Operations Domain**.

It is authoritative for accepted-student arrival procedures only when supported by current operational confirmation. It must not redefine visa law, academic rules, accommodation facts, insurance policy, or Safety & Support responsibilities.

Apply `21_CROSS_SPEC_GOVERNANCE.md`. A detected factual conflict is a publication blocker.

### Canonical Fact Certainty

Use `Confirmed`, `Provisional`, `Unknown`, and `Not Applicable`. Use `Subject to change` only for a real provisional plan.

## 1. Purpose

The Before You Arrive experience supports students from acceptance through arrival in Japan.

It should reduce avoidable uncertainty and help accepted students arrive prepared, informed, and confident.

Students should understand:

- what must be completed before departure,
- documents and entry requirements,
- payment and insurance status,
- accommodation and check-in,
- academic preparation,
- what to bring,
- medication/health preparation,
- arrival and first-day procedures,
- how to report travel changes,
- support routes and personal responsibilities.

**Prepared before departure. Confident on arrival.**

## 2. Truth Standard

Never invent or infer:

- arrival procedure,
- airport route/pickup,
- check-in time/location,
- accommodation assignment,
- insurance conditions,
- emergency contacts,
- visa/entry requirements,
- medication rules,
- packing requirements,
- transport costs,
- exact future weather,
- first-day details.

Previous-year instructions are reference material only until re-verified.

Do not make a guide look complete by filling gaps with plausible guesses.

## 3. Student Outcome

A student should be able to say:

> I know what I need to complete before I travel.  
> I know where I will stay.  
> I know how arrival is supposed to work.  
> I know what documents and insurance I need.  
> I know what I should bring.  
> I know what to do if my travel plans change.  
> I know how to contact the programme.  
> I know what happens when I arrive.

## 4. Information Layers

Use layered content:

1. **Accepted Student Overview** — concise must-do actions.
2. **Detailed Pre-Arrival Guide** — full preparation guidance.
3. **Accommodation / Arrival Detail** — property and arrival-specific instructions.
4. **Emergency Quick Reference** — concise urgent actions/contacts.

Do not force all information into one giant page or PDF.

## 5. Pre-Arrival Checklist

A checklist may include only real requirements such as:

- accept programme place,
- complete payment,
- confirm passport details,
- verify entry requirements,
- arrange/confirm insurance,
- submit arrival details,
- review accommodation/check-in,
- prepare medication,
- set up required communication tool,
- complete academic preparation,
- review emergency contacts.

A static page must not pretend to know personal completion status. If status cannot be verified, present guidance rather than fake progress tracking.

## 6. Acceptance / Payment / Outstanding Conditions

Explain actual acceptance-confirmation steps, due dates, payment requirements, and remaining conditions.

Do not label a place fully confirmed while material conditions remain outstanding.

Follow `18_BEFORE_YOU_APPLY_SPEC.md` for payment and application-domain rules.

## 7. Passport / Visa / Entry Requirements

Passport, visa, immigration, health-entry, and customs requirements are time-sensitive and applicant-specific.

Do not provide legal or immigration advice from memory.

Use current authoritative government sources and distinguish programme documents from immigration approval.

Do not say `You do not need a visa` unless verified for the relevant student context.

## 8. Programme Visa Support

If the programme provides an acceptance letter, programme confirmation, or other immigration-support document, state exactly what is provided.

Do not say the programme arranges or guarantees a visa unless literally true.

## 9. Insurance

Students should know the verified requirement, provider/arrangement responsibility, coverage period, proof deadline, policy access, and emergency use process where applicable.

Follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`. Do not over-summarise policy coverage.

## 10. Health Preparation and Medication

Pre-arrival information may encourage advance planning for regular medication, ongoing treatment, allergies, accessibility needs, or other participation considerations.

Do not give individual medical advice.

Do not assume a medicine is legal to import, a foreign prescription works in Japan, or the same product is available. Use current authoritative Japanese guidance for regulated medication/import questions.

Use confidential channels for sensitive information.

## 11. Accessibility Before Arrival

Where appropriate encourage early discussion of participation/access needs.

Do not promise every accommodation/modification. Explain the real case-by-case process.

## 12. Academic Preparation and Placement

Explain only real preparatory requirements such as script review, readings, online orientation, forms, or placement testing.

If placement occurs before arrival, explain timing, platform, deadline, purpose, and consequence of non-completion where verified.

Follow `13_SYLLABUS_SPEC.md`.

## 13. Required Devices / Technology

State only actual requirements for laptop, smartphone, headphones, charger, software, or internet access.

Do not assume every student has a laptop or unrestricted access to paid apps/software.

## 14. Electricity / Connectivity

Provide electrical/adapter guidance only when verified and useful.

For SIM/eSIM/roaming/Wi-Fi, distinguish what the programme provides from what students arrange themselves.

Do not present a commercial provider as the official recommendation without an approved reason/relationship.

## 15. Communication Channels

If the programme requires email, messaging app, emergency app, or another platform, explain what it is used for and when it must be set up.

Do not list channels that are not monitored.

Students should monitor the actual email/account used for programme communication.

## 16. Emergency Communication

Before departure, students should know the verified official urgent/emergency route.

Do not publish unverified personal numbers. Follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`.

## 17. Accommodation Confirmation

Before travel, students should receive confirmed information appropriate to the offering, such as:

- property/type,
- address/location as appropriate,
- room arrangement,
- check-in date/window,
- key rules/inclusions,
- how to reach the property.

Follow `15_ACCOMMODATION_SPEC.md`.

## 18. Accommodation Assignment and Roommates

Do not tell students their exact property, room, or roommate before assignment is confirmed.

Do not share another student's personal information without appropriate permission.

Do not promise pre-arrival roommate contact unless it is part of the real process.

## 19. Check-In

Confirmed check-in guidance should explain:

- date,
- time window,
- location,
- key/reception procedure,
- what to bring,
- what to do if delayed.

Do not reuse previous-year check-in instructions without confirmation.

## 20. Early Arrival / Late Departure / Missed Check-In

Clearly state whether students must arrange their own accommodation outside the official accommodation period.

Do not promise early check-in, late extension, baggage storage, or late arrival flexibility unless confirmed.

If missing the check-in window has material consequences, communicate this before travel.

## 21. Arrival Information Collection

Collect only operationally necessary flight/train/arrival details and explain how updates should be reported.

## 22. Airport Pickup

State airport-pickup status explicitly where students may reasonably expect it.

Do not imply pickup exists unless formally provided.

## 23. Arrival Routes

Provide practical current guidance from relevant airports/stations to accommodation/campus where useful.

Routes must be verified against current transport information before publication. Do not present one route as mandatory unless it is.

Avoid fragile timetable-specific instructions in evergreen content when a current route source is safer.

## 24. Public Transport and Taxi

Explain major transfer points, modes, approximate route structure, or walking where useful.

Do not promise exact travel times, taxi fares, vehicle availability, card acceptance, or luggage capacity without current evidence.

## 25. Luggage

Explain only real programme/property expectations around carrying luggage, storage, and forwarding.

Do not assume programme staff transport luggage.

If luggage forwarding is mentioned, distinguish a commercial independent service from a programme-arranged service.

## 26. Money and Personal Budget

Students may need practical guidance on cash/cards/ATMs and personal expenses.

Do not make absolute claims such as `Japan is cashless` or `Cash is always necessary`.

Do not fabricate a daily spending budget. Any estimate must explain its scope and basis.

Avoid permanent exchange-rate examples unless intentionally maintained and clearly derived.

## 27. Weather / Climate

Use seasonal practical guidance, not false long-range precision.

Do not write a specific future temperature months in advance. Direct students to a current forecast close to departure when weather matters.

## 28. Packing Guidance

Distinguish:

- **Required** — genuinely necessary.
- **Recommended** — practical advice.
- **Personal Choice** — optional comfort items.

Do not turn recommendations into mandatory requirements or create exhaustive shopping lists without value.

## 29. Clothing

State real activity-specific requirements such as walking shoes, formal attire, or workshop clothing only when relevant.

Seasonal clothing guidance should remain flexible because actual weather varies.

Do not invent cultural dress rules.

## 30. Documents to Carry

List only real needs such as passport, programme confirmation, accommodation information, insurance details, or other required documents.

Do not require printed copies when digital versions are accepted unless the process actually requires print.

## 31. Customs / Food / Restricted Items

Do not provide import/customs legality from memory.

Use current official sources for food, medication, restricted goods, or other regulated items.

## 32. Academic Materials

Before departure, students should know verified requirements for textbooks/materials/devices/stationery and whether items are provided or purchased.

## 33. Programme Schedule Before Arrival

Distinguish:

- confirmed schedule,
- draft/indicative schedule,
- first-day/orientation information.

Do not present a draft as final.

## 34. First Day

Where confirmed explain meeting place/time, what to bring, approximate duration, what happens, and what to do if late.

Make first-day instructions especially easy to find.

## 35. Orientation

Only list content actually covered, such as programme overview, campus, accommodation, safety, communication, academics, interaction, or daily life.

## 36. Campus Access / Accounts / Facilities

If students need IDs, passes, accounts, Wi-Fi setup, library/facility access, or registrations, explain the real process.

Do not imply full degree-student access if short-term participants do not receive it. Never publish sensitive credentials.

## 37. Student Interaction Before Arrival

State pre-arrival buddy/peer contact only if the programme actually supports it.

Do not create expectations of pre-arrival friendship. Follow `14_STUDENT_INTERACTION_SPEC.md`.

## 38. Social Media / Messaging Groups

Do not require unofficial social groups unless formally part of programme communication.

If optional, label them optional. Do not publicly expose private group links that should be controlled.

## 39. Programme Rules Before Arrival

Material rules around attendance, accommodation, conduct, communication, safety, and required activities should be accessible before arrival where practical.

Do not reveal major participation rules for the first time at check-in.

## 40. Attendance and Conduct

Attendance requirements must match `13_SYLLABUS_SPEC.md` and approved programme rules.

Conduct language should focus on safety, respect, participation, accommodation, and institutional policy—not vague moralising.

Do not introduce stricter rules at orientation without an approved basis.

## 41. Alcohol / Smoking

Distinguish verified legal, programme, and accommodation rules. Do not provide general legal claims from memory.

## 42. Free Time and Independent Travel

Explain whether students may make personal plans during genuine free time and distinguish programme responsibility from independent travel responsibility.

Do not imply continuous supervision or unrestricted absence from required activities.

## 43. Emergency Preparation

Before arrival students should know verified emergency-service action, programme urgent contact, insurance contact, and what to do if travel changes.

Follow `16_SAFETY_SUPPORT_SPEC.md`.

## 44. Offline Access

Where practical, critical arrival/emergency information should be retainable offline because data, Wi-Fi, or battery may be unavailable.

Possible formats include a saved page/PDF or compact reference, depending on real operations.

## 45. Travel Delay / Cancellation

Students should know how to report flight/train delay, missed connection, late check-in, or other material travel changes.

Do not direct them to an unmonitored channel.

Do not promise programme rebooking assistance unless it exists; airline/ticket arrangements generally remain the traveller's responsibility unless the programme explicitly provides otherwise.

## 46. International Neutrality and Time Zones

Do not assume all students depart from one country or airport.

Specify the time zone for deadlines/meeting times where ambiguity matters.

## 47. Contact Changes and Privacy

Explain how students should update phone, email, arrival details, or passport information where programme operations require it.

Handle travel, health, passport, and contact data according to approved privacy processes. Do not publish arrival lists unnecessarily.

## 48. Minors / Accompanying Staff / Families

If minors participate, pre-arrival consent, supervision, accommodation, travel, and emergency arrangements must be formally defined.

Do not assume accompanying staff are responsible for all personal/emergency support unless formally agreed.

The primary pre-arrival experience remains student-facing. Family information may summarise real support/communication boundaries without disclosing private student information.

## 49. Partner-Institution Layer

Partner institutions may need group arrival, participant-list, invoice, staff accommodation, or emergency-liaison information.

Keep institutional operations separate from student-facing guidance when this improves clarity.

## 50. Pre-Arrival FAQ

Useful questions may include:

- When should I arrive?
- Is airport pickup provided?
- When will I receive accommodation details?
- What if my flight is delayed?
- What should I bring?
- Do I need cash?
- What insurance do I need?
- Can I bring my medication?
- What happens on the first day?
- How do I contact the programme outside normal hours?

Answer only with current verified information and follow `20_FAQ_SPEC.md`.

## 51. Tone

Practical, calm, welcoming, precise, and confidence-building.

Avoid overexcited tourism copy, vague reassurance, intimidating policy tone, and patronising advice.

Create anticipation using real campus/programme/class/orientation/interaction facts rather than emotional promises.

## 52. UI Structure

A strong interface may use:

1. Welcome / current stage
2. Must-do checklist
3. Documents and entry
4. Payment / insurance
5. Accommodation
6. Arrival
7. Academic preparation
8. Packing / daily life
9. Safety / contacts
10. First day
11. FAQ

Do not make every section a card.

## 53. Student-Specific Status / Personalisation

Only show payment confirmed, insurance received, arrival details received, accommodation assigned, etc. when the system actually knows the status.

Do not fake personal status on an unauthenticated/static site.

Personalisation may use known programme/accommodation/applicant/arrival data where operationally justified, but must not infer sensitive traits or add unnecessary complexity.

## 54. Mobile / Accessibility / Performance

At 390px, accepted students should quickly find next required action, important deadline, accommodation status, arrival instructions, and emergency contact. At 320px remain fully usable.

Use semantic headings, keyboard-accessible checklists, meaningful links, readable alerts, sufficient contrast, and text beyond colour for completion/urgency.

Prioritise reliability on mobile networks over heavy media or dashboards.

## 55. Multilingual Consistency

Language versions may adapt explanation but must not differ in deadlines, check-in, insurance, accommodation, required documents, emergency contacts, or arrival instructions.

## 56. Source of Truth and Verification

Use current accepted-student instructions, programme data, accommodation confirmation, insurer information, current government guidance, approved syllabus, and Safety & Support procedures as appropriate.

Do not treat old email templates as authority when newer approved information exists.

Where practical track owner, source, last verified date, offering, audience, contact validity, accommodation status, and publication state.

## 57. Previous-Year Reuse

Re-verify accommodation, check-in, arrival routes, contacts, insurance, communication tools, first-day schedule, payment guidance, academic preparation, and emergency information.

Do not roll old instructions forward silently.

## 58. Publication Lifecycle

Use:

1. `Draft`
2. `Fact Check Required`
3. `Review`
4. `Ready for Publication`
5. `Published`
6. `Archived`

Possible blocking review flags:

- `Contact Verification Required`
- `Provider Confirmation Required`
- `Operational Review Required`
- `External Source Recheck Required`

Do not release student-critical pre-arrival information while arrival/contact details remain unresolved.

## 59. Change Control and Direct Notification

Material changes include check-in location/time, accommodation, emergency contact, communication tool, first-day meeting point, visa/entry guidance, insurance, required documents, and academic preparation.

Review affected pre-arrival, FAQ, accommodation, safety, programme, and student communication content.

If accepted students are already affected, **updating the website alone may not be sufficient**. Use the approved direct communication channel where required. Do not assume they will revisit the page.

## 60. Operational Dry Run / Scenario Review

Before arrival, consider testing current arrival instructions, check-in contact, emergency contact, accommodation contact, first-day meeting point, and communication platform.

Review realistic scenarios such as on-time arrival, early arrival, delay, missed check-in, lost phone, no mobile data, unreachable accommodation contact, and changed first-day location.

The purpose is to find unclear responsibility before students arrive.

## 61. Quality Review Checklist

### Truth
- dates, accommodation, check-in, contacts, insurance, routes, and current-offering details are verified.

### Student Preparation
- required actions/documents, academic preparation, and travel-change procedure are clear.

### Arrival
- airport pickup status, routes, delay handling, check-in, and first day are practical and current.

### Health / Safety
- medication/entry guidance uses authoritative sources; support needs are confidential; emergency information is verified and retainable offline where appropriate.

### Accommodation
- assignment, sharing, check-in/out, and early/late limitations are clear.

### UX / Governance
- must-do content is scannable on mobile, alerts are proportionate, current owner/source/offering/release state are known, and material changes will be pushed to affected students where needed.

## 62. Definition of Done

Before You Arrive is complete when accepted students know next actions, deadlines, documents, insurance, entry-information boundaries, accommodation/check-in, arrival route, academic preparation, packing status, medication-information source, emergency contact, delay procedure, and first-day expectations—and all are current for the offering.

## 63. Final Standard

A student should be able to say:

> I know what I need to do before I leave home.  
> I know what I need to bring.  
> I know where I am going.  
> I know how check-in works.  
> I know what to do if my journey changes.  
> I know how to get help.  
> I am arriving prepared, not guessing.

The goal is not to remove every uncertainty from international travel.

**The goal is to remove every uncertainty the programme itself can reasonably prevent.**
