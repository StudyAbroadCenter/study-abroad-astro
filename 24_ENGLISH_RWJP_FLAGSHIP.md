# English RWJP Flagship Standard

Status: implementation baseline for English programme-detail localisation.

## Purpose

RWJP is the first English programme-detail page and the reference model for later English programme pages. It is not a sentence-by-sentence translation of the Japanese page.

The Japanese Master remains the source for verified programme meaning and the canonical data modules remain the source for changeable facts. English owns its editorial rhythm, headings, calls to action, typography and page composition.

## Editorial standard

- Write for an international university student reading an English-language university website.
- Prefer concrete verbs and specific programme value over generic study-abroad advertising language.
- Do not use Japanese wordplay literally when it produces unnatural English.
- Keep institutional facts calm and precise; allow more personality in student-facing headings.
- Avoid unsupported promises about transformation, fluency, friendship, outcomes or access.
- Distinguish organised programme elements from participant free time.
- State annual uncertainty where arrangements can vary.

## RWJP English narrative

Core idea: **Study Japanese. Live Kyoto.**

This is an English editorial interpretation of the Japanese concept that five weeks combines serious Japanese study with the experience of living in Kyoto. It is not a claim that the programme provides a complete residential or tourism experience.

Supporting narrative:

1. Five weeks gives participants time to develop routines rather than only visit Kyoto.
2. Japanese language study is the academic centre of RWJP.
3. Student interaction and cultural activities add contexts in which participants can encounter Japanese beyond exercises.
4. Free time belongs to the participant and must not be represented as an organised itinerary.
5. Eligibility, dates, fees, accommodation and application rules come from canonical facts, not English copy.

## Design standard

The English page may differ from Japanese in composition. It should use:

- stronger editorial typography;
- shorter English headings;
- generous whitespace;
- a restrained university palette;
- photography as narrative evidence rather than decoration;
- scannable decision information;
- mobile layouts designed for English line length rather than inherited Japanese breaks.

## Multilingual reuse

Future English programme pages should reuse this page as a quality model, not as a rigid visual template. Korean, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese localisation must return to canonical facts and programme meaning rather than translating this English copy.

A fact change must be made in the authoritative data source and then verified across every published locale. Localised prose must never become a second factual source.
